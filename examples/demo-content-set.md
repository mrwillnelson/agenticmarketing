## Posts

### Post 1: Paying 85 grand a month not to look
Source: 05:24 to 06:46

I signed off on the same ad budget for eight straight quarters.

Eighty-five thousand dollars a month. Two platforms, an agency, a green dashboard.

I never once opened the cohort math myself.

Nobody audits a green dashboard. Red numbers get meetings. Green numbers get budget increases.

When I finally built the sheet, blended CAC said 1,900 dollars. Underneath it, the paid-only cohorts were costing 3,400 dollars a customer, and they churned at nearly double the rate of the referred ones.

And then I kept the channel alive for six more months. Killing it felt like admitting I had burned a year of runway.

That was ego, not strategy.

Two quarters after we shut it off: CAC at 640 dollars. Same product, same team, same market.

The lesson is not that paid is bad. It is that whoever reports the number cannot be the only one computing the number.

## Newsletter section

### The dark quarter
Source: 06:49 to 08:07

There is a quarter in this story nobody puts in the highlight reel.

Meridian shut off ninety percent of paid spend in one quarter, on the strength of a spreadsheet and a theory. Then the theory got tested: "Pipeline dipped thirty percent almost immediately" (07:20). Two investors called separately to ask, very politely, if Dana had lost her mind.

What held the line was not conviction. It was the cohort sheet: paid-only cohorts at thirty-four hundred dollars a customer, twenty-six month payback on twelve month contracts, churn near double the referred cohorts. When the panic came, the math did not move.

Pipeline caught the old baseline in month four and passed it in month six, with about half the funnel volume and double the close rate.

If you are about to kill a channel you no longer believe in, budget for the dark quarter. It is the toll the decision charges, and it is temporary. Funding churn is not.
