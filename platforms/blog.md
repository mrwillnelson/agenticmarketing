# Blog Constraints

## The fold

- The title and the first paragraph are the fold. Both follow the full hook discipline; a reader who bounces there never sees the rest.
- Write the title as a hook, not a label. "How we think about pricing" is a label; a title states or provokes the tension.
- The first paragraph is a second hook, not a preamble. Never open with definitions, history, or "in today's landscape".
- Pay the title's promise inside the first 100 words. A bait title with a slow open doubles the bounce.
- One thesis per post. Side ideas become their own posts.

## Structure for skimmers

- Write descriptive subheads that tell the story alone. A skimmer reading only the subheads should get the argument's spine.
- "Introduction", "Background", and "Conclusion" are dead subheads. Every subhead makes a claim or names a turn.
- Keep paragraphs short and sections single-purpose. A section either moves the thesis forward or gets cut.
- Front-load each section: first sentence carries the section's point, the rest proves it.
- Break long sections with one concrete element: an example, a number, or a pulled quote. Never a stock image.
- Use bullets and numbered lists for genuinely parallel items, not to fake structure.
- Transitions carry tension into the next section. Never "Additionally" or "Moreover".
- End when the idea is done. No summary section, no "in conclusion" recap.

## SEO without stuffing

- Target one search intent per post. A post chasing two queries ranks for neither.
- Place the keyword where it reads naturally: the title, one subhead, and the first 100 words. That is the whole placement plan.
- Write the meta description as a second hook, ~155 characters. It is the fold in search results.
- Keep the URL slug short and readable, keyword included, no dates.
- Never keyword-stuff a thought-leadership piece. Forced repetition reads as spam to readers and increasingly to search engines.
- If a keyword will not fit a sentence naturally, rewrite the sentence or drop the keyword. The reader outranks the crawler.
- Prefer internal links over external for keeping readers. Link to your own related posts where they genuinely extend the point.
- Use external links only when they carry proof the post depends on. Every outbound link is an exit.
- Anchor text describes the destination. Never "click here".
- Open external links in context, not as a wall of references. Proof belongs next to the claim it supports.
- A thought-leadership post that reads well ranks longer than one optimized to death. Write for the reader first.
