# LinkedIn Constraints

## The fold and the feed

- Only the first ~210 characters show before "...see more" on mobile. They must work alone as a hook.
- Never spend the fold on setup, context, or throat-clearing. Tension first, explanation after the click.
- Break the body into paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences. Readers see a narrow phone column, not a document.
- Reread every draft at phone width. A paragraph that reads fine on desktop is a wall on a phone.
- End on the strongest plain sentence, then delete everything after it.
- Hard platform limit: 3,000 characters. Stay far below it; length is earned, not filled.

## Reach mechanics

- The algorithm rewards dwell time over clicks. Native text that holds a reader beats a teaser that sends them away.
- Links in the post are fine but cost reach. Include one when the link is the point; cut it when it is decoration.
- "Link in comments" is a dated tactic. Do not use it as a reach hack.
- No engagement bait: no "Agree?", no "Thoughts?", no polls-for-reach, no "comment KEYWORD and I'll DM you".
- Hashtags add little. Zero is the default; use them only when the account's approved posts already do.
- Tag people only when they will plausibly respond. Tags that get ignored hurt distribution.
- Edits shortly after posting are fine. Fix the typo; do not delete and repost.

## Formats

Text post:

- The default format. Hook above the fold, short paragraphs, strongest line last.
- No fake punchline endings and no corporate CTAs. The post ends when the idea ends.

Carousel/document:

- The cover slide is the hook. It carries the whole stop-the-scroll burden, same discipline as a text-post hook.
- One idea per slide, 8 to 12 slides total. A slide that needs two slides is two ideas or it is bloated.
- Every slide must survive as a standalone screenshot: a claim plus its support, never a fragment.
- The caption frames the carousel and creates a reason to swipe. It never summarizes the slides.

Video:

- Captions on, always. Most of the feed watches muted.
- The first 3 seconds decide the scroll. Open mid-tension, never mid-introduction.
