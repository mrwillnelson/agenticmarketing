# Video Constraints

## The 3-second hook

- The first 3 seconds decide the scroll. That is roughly 8 spoken words; they must create tension alone.
- Open mid-thought, never mid-introduction. No "hey", no names, no "in this video".
- The hook must also work muted: on-screen text or visible tension carries it when the sound is off.
- The second line pays the hook's promise or opens the loop wider. A hook followed by setup is an exit at 8 seconds.
- Write for the ear. Every line must survive being said out loud in one breath; written-register sentences die on camera.
- Use contractions and short sentences. Nobody says "do not" out loud.

## Retention and length

- Attention dips every 8 to 10 seconds. Place a new open loop, a turn, or a visual change at each dip; never let two flat lines run back to back.
- Pick the length band by the job, then hold one unit of content per band:
  - 15 to 30 seconds: one beat. A single claim, moment, or number that lands and ends.
  - 30 to 60 seconds: one story. Setup, turn, payoff; nothing else fits.
  - 60 to 90 seconds: one argument. A claim, its proof, and the landing.
- Structure scripts as marked beats: hook, turn, payoff. Each beat on its own line.
- A clip with two points is two clips. Cut, do not compress.
- End when the idea lands. No "follow for more", no "link in bio", no outro.

## Delivery and packaging

- Captions on, always. Most of the feed watches muted; a clip without captions forfeits those viewers.
- Default to vertical 9:16. Horizontal footage gets reframed or cropped for the feed, not letterboxed.
- Keep captions and on-screen text out of the platform UI bands at the top and bottom of the frame.
- On-screen text mirrors the spoken hook in the first 3 seconds. After that, reserve it for numbers and key phrases.
- When cutting from a recording, keep the speaker's exact words. Never script or smooth what they said; energy beats polish.
- Mark exact in and out timestamps for every cut, and open the clip on the tension, not the wind-up.
- The caption or description that accompanies a clip frames it: it sets up why the moment matters and leaves the payoff to the clip.
- Never summarize or transcribe the clip in its caption. A caption that spoils the clip removes the reason to press play.
- The caption obeys its host platform's fold rules. A clip posted to LinkedIn gets a LinkedIn caption; check that module.
- One caption per clip. Do not reuse a caption across platforms without re-checking each fold.
