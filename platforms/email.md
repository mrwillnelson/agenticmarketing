# Email Constraints

## The inbox fold

- The fold is a two-part hook: subject line plus preview text, read together in one glance.
- Keep the subject to ~50 visible characters. Most inboxes truncate after that; front-load the tension.
- Keep the preview text to ~90 visible characters. Write it deliberately; never let the client scrape "View in browser".
- The preview must complete the subject, not repeat it. A repeated preview wastes half the fold.
- Specific beats clever. A subject that survives an inbox with 50 unread emails names something concrete.
- No "Newsletter #47" subjects. The issue number is not a hook.
- The subject promises; the first line of the body must start paying. A bait-and-switch earns the next delete-on-sight.

## The body

- Write to one reader. "Hey everyone" and "you all" break the illusion that makes email work.
- Use a single column. Multi-column layouts break on phones and read as marketing, not a message.
- Make it scannable: short paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences, subheads that carry meaning, one thesis per issue.
- Add subheads only when the issue runs long. A short email reads as a single flow.
- Keep formatting plain: bold sparingly, one image at most, no banner header. Designed emails read as marketing.
- Use personalization tokens only when they change the sentence's meaning. "{FirstName}, quick question" reads as automation.
- Sections either move the thesis forward or get cut. An email is not three unrelated thoughts stapled together.
- The email register is warmer and more direct than a feed post. Write like a message, not a broadcast.
- End reply-worthy: a real question or a sharp claim. One CTA at most; never a stack of buttons.
- A reply is worth more than a click. Replies train inbox providers to keep you out of spam.

## Deliverability that shapes the writing

- Text-forward bodies land; image-heavy bodies drift to promotions or spam. Write the message, do not design it.
- Every link raises the spam score a little. Include only links that earn their place.
- Keep the from-name consistent across every send. Readers open the sender before they read the subject, and filters score sender reputation the same way.
- Never send from a no-reply address. It kills replies and signals broadcast to both readers and filters.
- Avoid spam-adjacent phrasing in subjects: all caps, multiple exclamation marks, "free", "act now".
- Avoid link shorteners. Filters treat them as a masking signal; use the plain destination URL.
- Attachments belong on a link, never in the send. Attached files are a classic spam trigger.
