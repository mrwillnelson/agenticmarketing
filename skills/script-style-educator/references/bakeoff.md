# Bake-off: the lecture vs the lesson

One lesson, two scripts. The lesson is real: how 64stories grew an established CEO's LinkedIn account 500% in 90 days, from Will Nelson's published post of 2026-03-12. The mechanics below (biweekly idea calls, shaping posts together, boosting winners as thought leader ads, scraping engagement for sales) all come from that post. Both scripts target the 30 to 60 second band from `short-form-script`.

## Baseline: the lecture-style script

> Hey everyone. Today I want to talk about something that's really important for founders in 2026, and that's executive presence on LinkedIn.

Nine seconds gone and nothing promised. "Today I want to talk about" is banned throat-clearing, and "executive presence" is a topic, not an ability. The parent skill's 3-second fold is already blown.

> There are six key pillars you need to keep in mind. First, define your personal brand. Second, optimize your profile. Third, maintain a consistent posting cadence.

Six steps means the lesson was too big, and these are not even steps. "Define your personal brand" has no visible finish line; a viewer cannot tell when they have done it.

> Fourth, engage authentically with your community. Fifth, leverage analytics to understand what resonates. And sixth, be patient, because growth takes time.

Manual, not scar. Nothing here required the speaker to have done anything. Any account that has never grown a CEO's audience could read these same lines, and the viewer can feel that.

> Experts agree that consistency is the number one factor, and studies show that personal accounts outperform company pages.

Borrowed authority in place of proof. Which experts, which studies, whose account. The speaker runs this exact motion for clients and cites none of it.

> So those are my six pillars for executive presence. Drop a comment with your biggest LinkedIn challenge, and follow for part two where we go deeper on each pillar.

A recap plus an outro, both banned by the parent. And "part two where we go deeper" is the confession: six pillars never fit one script. There is no line here a viewer could repeat to a colleague, because nothing was actually taught.

## With the skill: the educator script

> We grew a CEO's LinkedIn 500% in 90 days. Three steps, and you can run them Monday.

The promise: a real measured result, then the ability and the deadline. Nine spoken words to the fold. The number is from the founder's own published work, which is what hard rule 2 demands.

> Step one. Every two weeks, get on a call and bring the post ideas yourself. Never ask a CEO what they want to share. You propose, they react.

An action with a finish line: a call happened, ideas were proposed. The second sentence is the curse of knowledge check paying off. "Hold an ideation call" hides the part that makes it work; a smart outsider would ask the CEO an open question and get nothing.

> Step two. Shape each idea together until it's true and sounds like him. If he wouldn't say the line out loud at dinner, cut it.

The founder's private heuristic handed over, not a principle. "Make it authentic" is the manual version; the dinner test is the scar version, and it is executable.

> Step three. Publish, wait, and only put ad money behind posts that already took off. Winners get boosted. Everything else stays organic.

Falls at the parent's retention dip and opens with a small reversal: spend comes after performance, not before. Three steps, done. The temptation was five; the measurement loop got banked as its own script.

> That loop took an established account up 500% in 90 days. And every like on those posts gets scraped and sent to sales.

The proof beat: the promise's number restated now that the viewer knows what earned it, plus the detail that shows this is a working system, not a growth trick. Both facts are from the source post, exact.

> Company milestones, in the CEO's voice, boosted only after they earn it.

The takeaway, twelve words, repeatable to a colleague without the video. Then hard cut, per the parent. No recap, no follow ask.

## What changed, in five lines

- A topic ("executive presence") became an ability with a deadline ("run it Monday").
- Six unfinishable pillars became three actions, each with its step 1.5 spoken out loud.
- "Experts agree" became one exact number from the founder's own measured work.
- Advice from nowhere became a system the founder actually runs, dinner test included.
- The recap-and-follow outro became a twelve-word takeaway and a hard cut.

The lecture script is not wrong anywhere. It is unfalsifiable everywhere, which is worse: nothing in it could only have been said by someone who did the work. The educator script cannot be delivered by anyone else, and that is the whole style.
