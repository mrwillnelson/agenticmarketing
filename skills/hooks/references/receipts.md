# Receipts: Will Nelson, Founder of 64stories.com

Real openers from Will's own LinkedIn posts, with real engagement numbers. Use them to calibrate what a passing finalist looks like: each one names its emotional lens and leaves exactly one question open.

## "Ask a founder about storytelling and they'll point you to their marketing team."

141 reactions, 35 comments. 79 characters, whole hook above the LinkedIn fold.

- Lens: Me too, tipping into Oh shit. Every founder has done exactly this, and the framing implies the behavior is evidence of something.
- Open question: what does delegating the story reveal about the founder? The hook shows the behavior and withholds the diagnosis. The next line, "That tells you everything.", deepens the gap instead of closing it.
- Why it passes the deployment gate: an audience-belief mirror with no invented drama, and it could not be pasted onto a different post.

## "This guy broke LinkedIn, so let's figure out what he did so you can copy it."

117 reactions, 22 comments. 76 characters above the fold.

- Lens: Hell yeah. The reader is promised a copyable result before the subject is even named.
- Open question: who is he and what did he do? The receipt, 5,000 followers to 52,000 almost overnight, lands in line two, so the hook holds only the identity and the mechanism open.
- Note the economy: no name spent in the first line. The withheld subject is the curiosity gap.

## "The fastest way to tell a company is drifting."

51 reactions, 13 comments, on a post of only 37 words. 46 characters above the fold.

- Lens: Oh shit. A diagnostic the reader immediately wants to self-administer.
- Open question: what is the test? Answered one line later, "Ask five leaders to explain what the company does.", which opens the next gap in turn: what will I hear?
- Shows a hook needs neither a number nor a confession. A withheld test is enough voltage to carry a very short post.

What the three share: each survives the fold alone, names one emotion, and leaves exactly one question open. None of them closes its own loop.
