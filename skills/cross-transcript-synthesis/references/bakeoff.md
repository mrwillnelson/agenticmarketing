# Bake-off: per-call summaries versus cross-transcript synthesis

The same three transcript excerpts run through a baseline (one summary per call, the best any single-call tool can do) and through this skill. The baseline is locally accurate three times and still misses the story. The skill catches it.

All three excerpts are constructed examples, written for this bake-off. Will Nelson, founder of 64stories.com, is a real person; these specific conversations never happened. The themes echo his real published writing.

## Inputs: three constructed excerpts spanning three months

### Excerpt A: prospect discovery call, 2026-01-14 (constructed)

> Prospect: So what should our CEO actually be doing on LinkedIn?
> Will: Honestly, the founders who win just post more. Volume is the whole game. You show up daily for ninety days and the compounding takes care of the rest.
> Prospect: And how does that tie to pipeline? Like, revenue?
> Will: Engagement compounds and pipeline follows. It always lags, but it follows. And look, if you can't clearly explain what your company does in one line, that's not a writing problem, that's a strategy problem. We fix that first, then we turn up the volume. The account we started in December, impressions just keep climbing week over week.

### Excerpt B: podcast guest recording, 2026-02-19 (constructed)

> Host: You preach consistency a lot. Still the number one lever?
> Will: I keep saying the story and the strategy are the same problem, and I believe that more every month. But the cadence thing, I'm less sure about. We doubled posting for a quarter on one account and pipeline did not move, that one's been bugging me.
> Host: So posting more didn't do it. What did?
> Will: On a different account we grew an established CEO's following 500 percent in 90 days, and the difference wasn't volume. Every post rowed toward one narrative.
> Host: Okay, but how do you actually connect any of this to revenue?
> Will: That's the question everyone asks me. I'll have a better answer soon.

### Excerpt C: internal strategy call, 2026-03-26 (constructed)

> Teammate: The new deck still leads with posting cadence. Keep it?
> Will: No. I was wrong about volume. Cadence was never the lever, the narrative is the lever. We craft the story first and every post rows toward it, that's the offer now.
> Teammate: Clients will still ask how it reaches pipeline.
> Will: They ask every single time. So we show them the bridge: we scrape the likes and comments off every post and send them to sales to nurture, and we boost the winners as thought leader ads. The narrative-first account did 669,000 impressions in 28 days and the impressions are still climbing.

## Baseline: per-call summaries

- Summary A: Will advised a prospect that daily posting volume is the main lever for founder LinkedIn growth, said clarity of story reflects clarity of strategy, and noted a newer account's impressions rising.
- Summary B: Will discussed a case where doubling posting cadence did not move pipeline, shared a 500 percent follower growth result in 90 days driven by narrative alignment, and deferred a question on revenue attribution.
- Summary C: Will decided to reposition the company's offer around narrative-first content, described routing post engagement to sales and boosting winners as ads, and cited 669,000 impressions in 28 days.

Why the baseline misses: each summary is accurate and each would pass review. But the January belief and the March reversal live in different documents, so the highest-trust post available ("I was wrong about volume") is invisible. The pipeline question appears once per summary and reads as trivia instead of demand. The 500 percent and 669,000 receipts sit in separate files and never become one claim. No single call contained any of this; only the set does.

## Annotations

- The catch came from procedure step 1 (chronological inventory) plus step 4 (thresholds): three dated appearances of the volume position turned a vibe into an arc, and hard rule 5 is why the February doubt mattered as the third point.
- Hard rule 3 preserved "that one's been bugging me" intact; a cleaned-up paraphrase would have flattened the exact line that makes the post human.
- The prospect-versus-team gap went to the PRIVATE list, not to candidates. It is the most commercially urgent finding in the memo and the least publishable one, which is exactly why the two sections are separate.
- The demand signal reframes the attribution question from an annoyance into the next pillar: three different audiences asked it unprompted inside ten weeks.
