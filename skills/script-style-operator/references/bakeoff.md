# Bake-off: vague brag vs shown math

One metric story handled two ways. Every number below is real and Will's own, from his published LinkedIn posts of 2026-03-03 and 2026-03-12; nothing is invented. The story: founder content for a new 64stories client did 669,000 impressions in 28 days.

## The source material

From the 2026-03-03 post, verbatim:

> 669,000 impressions in 28 days from founder content on LinkedIn
>
> these are the results we're seeing from a new client at 64stories, and the impressions keep climbing – driving cost of acquisition lower and lower

> we're just capturing stories of the company and getting the founder to share them in his voice

> 1. Craft a great story and narrative
> 2. Create a process to capture stories inside and outside your company
> 3. Turn those story ideas into posts
> 4. Measure what works
> 5. Amplify your top performers with LinkedIn thought-leader ads

A second real number exists in the 2026-03-12 post: an established CEO's account grown 500% in 90 days. Different client, different mechanism. Watch what each version does with it.

## Baseline: the vague brag

No style module. The drafter grabs everything impressive and rounds up for effect.

| Spoken | Visual |
|---|---|
| "Our client just got absolutely massive results on LinkedIn." | Text overlay: MASSIVE RESULTS |
| "We're talking almost three quarters of a million impressions, basically overnight." | Fire emoji overlay |
| "And another client? Up five hundred percent. The growth has been insane." | Screenshot montage |
| "The secret is simple. The algorithm rewards authenticity, and we lean all the way in." | Founder nodding |
| "Founder-led content is the biggest opportunity in B2B right now. Don't sleep on it." | Text overlay: DON'T SLEEP ON IT |
| "Follow for more growth tips like this." | Point at follow button |

What happens: "almost three quarters of a million" rounds 669,000 up by 12%, and the one commenter who finds the original post now owns the thread. Two results from two different clients are stuffed into one script, so neither gets a mechanism and the parent's one-idea rule is broken twice. "The algorithm rewards authenticity" is a virtue dressed as an explanation; nobody can copy it, so nobody believes it. There is no time window spoken ("basically overnight" is false; it was 28 days), no math, no catch, and the outro plays to viewers who already left. An operator watching this files it under advertising and scrolls.

## With the skill: the shown math

One metric, 60 to 90 second band, one argument. The 500% story is banked as its own future script.

| Spoken | Visual |
|---|---|
| "Six hundred sixty nine thousand impressions in twenty eight days." | Overlay: 669,000 impressions / 28 days. On camera, mid-thought |
| "One founder's LinkedIn. I'll show you the mechanism, the math, and what it costs." | Hold on camera |
| "We didn't write him content. We captured stories his company already had, and he told them in his own voice." | B-roll: call recording, story doc |
| "The lever is one narrative. Sales, product, and every post rowing in unison, toward the same target." | Overlay: Craft > Capture > Create > Measure > Amplify |
| "Now do the math with me. Six hundred sixty nine thousand divided by twenty eight is about twenty three thousand impressions a day. Every day, for a month, from one account." | Overlay: 669,000 / 28 = 23,892 per day |
| "Here's the catch. Impressions aren't revenue. Some of that reach is paid on purpose, because when a post wins we boost it as a thought leader ad. And none of it happens if the founder stops showing up with stories." | Cut in tight |
| "So the real receipt isn't the six sixty nine. It's that his cost of acquisition keeps dropping while that number climbs." | Hold one beat, hard cut |

About 156 spoken words: inside the band.

## What changed, annotated

- **One metric.** The 500% in 90 days result is a different client and a different mechanism (milestones through the CEO's voice, biweekly calls). It became a second script instead of a second brag. The baseline spent both numbers and cashed neither.
- **Rounding direction.** The baseline rounded 669,000 up. The skill version speaks the exact figure, and rounds only the derived rate, downward: 23,892 a day spoken as "about twenty three thousand", with the exact division in the overlay.
- **Redoable math.** A viewer can divide 669,000 by 28 on a napkin and land within rounding of the spoken figure. That is the credibility inversion doing its work: the plain division is more persuasive than any adjective the baseline used.
- **Mechanism, not virtue.** "Captured stories his company already had, and he told them in his own voice" is an action a viewer could start copying tomorrow. "The algorithm rewards authenticity" is weather.
- **The catch sells the rest.** Naming the paid amplification (step 5 of the published process) and the founder-time dependency is exactly what makes the organic part believable. The script gives up a little shine and buys back all of its trust.
- **Time window spoken.** "Twenty eight days" is in the first breath. "Basically overnight" was both false and less impressive than the truth, which is the usual way vague brags lose twice.
- **Voice.** Long reasoning sentences mixed with short ones, one idea per line, no exclamation points, no rhetorical questions, and the ending stops a beat early on the line a viewer would repeat: acquisition cost falling while impressions climb, which is the claim the source post actually makes.
