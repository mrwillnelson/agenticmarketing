# Bake-off: CTA on everything vs one earned CTA

The same planned week handled two ways. The five posts below are invented for this exercise. The numbers that motivate the exercise are real, from Will Nelson's own LinkedIn feed.

## The real numbers first

Five of Will's published posts, February to March 2026, ordered by reactions:

| Post | Ending | Reactions | Comments |
|------|--------|-----------|----------|
| "Your story is your strategy, made legible" (2026-02-26) | Lands the thesis line, then a single link line below it: "Read the full piece:" | 141 | 35 |
| The five-leaders drift test (2026-03-04, 37 words) | "This article explains why." The link continues the exact promise | 51 | 13 |
| 669,000 impressions in 28 days for a 64stories client (2026-03-03) | Stacked asks: "let me know if you have questions", "happy to answer them below!", "follow me, Will Nelson, for everything founder-led growth" | 17 | 3 |
| A week-long teardown of one company's founder-led content engine (2026-03-25) | A keyword comment gate: comment the word and it gets sent, "(Must be connected)" | 14 | 15 |
| CEO account grown 500% in 90 days by 64stories (2026-03-12) | "Want this for your CEO? Get in touch!" | 11 | 2 |

Read it honestly, both ways:

- The two posts that ended clean, or with a link that continued the exact promise, carried 141 and 51 reactions. The three posts that ended in asks carried 17, 14, and 11. Small sample, real pattern: the asks cluster at the bottom.
- The comment-gate post is not simply a failure. 15 comments against 14 reactions means the gate did its mechanical job: it manufactured senders. Under hard rule 8 that post is judged on how many of those 15 were ICP-fit and what they converted to, not on the 14 reactions. The problem is not that one post gated a magnet. The problem is a feed where the asks outnumber the clean endings, so the reach posts and the ask posts are the same posts, and both jobs get done badly.

That is what the cap fixes: concentrate the ask into one earned slot and let the other posts buy the reach.

## The invented week

All five posts below are invented. The planner is working from this magnet map, also invented for the exercise:

- Pillar "Story is strategy": magnet is the Narrative Alignment Scorecard, a one-page test: ask five leaders "what does this company do", score the drift.
- Pillar "Founder-led distribution": no magnet yet.
- Pillar "Receipts" (client results): no magnet.

## Baseline: CTA on everything

Every post ends the same way:

> Comment STORY and I will send you my Narrative Alignment Scorecard (must be connected).
> Follow me, Will Nelson, for everything founder-led growth.

What happens, post by post:

- Mon: the story lands, then the ask reprices it. The room, the five answers, the silence, all of it becomes setup for a download. Readers who felt something now feel sold to.
- Tue: the only post where the offer actually fits, and it is indistinguishable from the other four, so the fit buys nothing.
- Wed: a reader finishes a post about hiring trends and is offered a scorecard about leadership drift. The subject changed at the exact moment the post should have stopped. Earn test failed in public.
- Thu: the receipts post already strains credulity for cold readers; a keyword gate on top reads as the whole reason the post exists.
- Fri: by the fifth identical ending, the audience has learned the account's shape. They stop reading the last block, then they stop reading the last third, because they know how it ends.

Five asks, one week. The scorecard gets some sends, mostly from peers who comment on everything. The feed's cleanest asset, endings that stop a beat early, is gone.

## With the skill: one carrier

Step 2 first: no funnel CTA ran in the previous window, so this week may carry one.

| Day | Story post? | Pillar has a magnet? | Earn test | Verdict |
|-----|-------------|----------------------|-----------|---------|
| Mon | Yes | Yes | not reached | Clean. Hard rule 4, no exceptions |
| Tue | No | Yes | Pass: the scorecard is the test the post just taught | Carrier |
| Wed | No | No | not reached | Clean. No magnet for this pillar, so nothing to offer |
| Thu | No | No | not reached | Clean. Receipts have no magnet, and the cap is spent anyway |
| Fri | No | Yes | Pass, but weaker: the post argues a position, it does not hand the reader a task | Clean. Cap allows one, and Tue's promise sits closer to the magnet |

Note the Fri tie-break: two posts passed the earn test. Tue wins because its reader finishes the post already wanting to run the test, and the scorecard is the test. Fri's reader finishes convinced, not equipped; a CTA there would pass the letter of the earn test and still feel like a swerve.

Tue's post ends on its strongest content line ("The score does not tell you what to fix. It tells you what to stop pretending is aligned."), then carries one of these blocks, chosen with the user after stating the trade:

Plain link, the default:

> The scorecard I use for this is here: [link]. One page, five questions, ten minutes.

Comment gate, if the user picks it knowing the cost:

> If you want the scorecard, comment SCORECARD and I will send it. I am asking for the comment because comments carry the post further; that is the trade.

Delete test on both: remove the block and Tuesday's post still works whole.

## The log

One entry goes to `my-story/what-worked.md`:

> CTA window w/o 2026-04-06: carrier was Tue (five-leaders test how-to), magnet Narrative Alignment Scorecard, form: plain link. Collect: link clicks, sends, ICP-fit share of requesters, replies, calls booked. Verdict on conversions only; reactions recorded for context. Next eligible window opens after 4 more posts.

Four clean posts buy the reach. One earned CTA spends it. The audience never learns to skip the ending, because most weeks the ending is the gift.
