# Lead Magnet CTA

Most feeds teach the audience one lesson fast: this account always ends in an ask, skip the ending. This skill decides which single post in a run earns a funnel CTA, holds the rest clean, and writes the offer as a continuation of the post instead of a toll booth at the end of it. A CTA is an offer the post earned, made once, after the post has done its whole job without it.

## Hard rules

1. The earn test. A post earns a funnel CTA only when the CTA continues the post's exact promise. A post about the CAC math can offer the CAC calculator. It cannot offer the newsletter; that changes the subject. If the reader would ask "what does this have to do with what I just read", the post did not earn it.
2. The frequency cap: at most one funnel CTA per 4 to 5 posts. Every-post CTAs train the audience to skip the ending, then the post, then the account. If a funnel CTA ran inside the current window, this post carries none, even if it passes the earn test.
3. Placement. The CTA sits after the post has fully landed, as its own block below the last line. It never replaces the ending; the `linkedin-post` ending law still owns the last content line. Delete test: remove the CTA and the post must still work completely. If deleting the CTA leaves a stump, the post was written as an ad.
4. Never bolt a CTA onto a story post. An ask after a story reprices the story as setup, retroactively. The story was the gift; leave it one.
5. A CTA is an offer, not bait. The `anti-ai` engagement-bait ban applies in full. "Comment X and I will send it" gates the magnet behind engagement mechanics the algorithm rewards; a plain link asks nothing performative but typically costs reach. Weigh the two honestly and state the reach cost to the user. Never dress a comment gate as generosity.
6. The magnet map: each pillar maps to at most one magnet. A post whose pillar has no matching magnet carries no funnel CTA, no matter how well the post performs. There is no universal magnet.
7. No magnet yet means build the magnet first. Never point a post at a placeholder, a "coming soon", or a magnet that does not exist. Say so plainly, then hand to `newsletter` and `founder-pitch` to work out what the magnet could be (see Related skills).
8. A CTA post is judged on pull-through, not reactions. Log clicks, requests, and conversions in `my-story/what-worked.md`. A CTA post with half the reactions and three booked calls beat the post with triple the likes.

## Procedure

1. Build or load the magnet map. Read `my-story/pillars.md` for the pillars and `my-story/narrative.md` for the funnel (what a post feeds, what that feeds next). For each pillar, record its one magnet or record "none". If the user has no magnet anywhere, stop at hard rule 7.
2. Check the window. List the last 4 to 5 published posts and the current week's plan. If any of them carried a funnel CTA, the cap is spent: every post in this pass ships clean, and the skill's job is done except for logging.
3. Find the one candidate. For each planned post, name its promise in one sentence, then apply the earn test against the pillar's magnet. Story posts are out before the test starts (hard rule 4). Posts on a pillar with no magnet are out (hard rule 6). If two posts pass, pick the one whose promise sits closest to the magnet and whose audience per `my-story/icp.md` is buyers, not peers.
4. Choose the form. Default to the plain link with the offer named in one line. If the user wants the comment gate, present both with the tradeoff stated: the gate usually reaches further and builds a senders list, but it makes readers perform in public to get a file and it borrows against trust. The user picks; the skill never picks the gate silently.
5. Place it. Finish the post so its last content line is the strongest beat. Then add the CTA as a separate block: one or two lines, plain, naming exactly what the reader gets and that it continues what they just read. Run the delete test.
6. Log for measurement. Note in `my-story/what-worked.md` which post carried the CTA, which magnet, which form, and the numbers to collect: link clicks or keyword comments, sends, replies, and conversions downstream. Reactions get recorded but never used as the verdict.

To see the whole discipline run against a week of posts, read [references/bakeoff.md](references/bakeoff.md): five invented posts, the CTA-on-everything baseline, the with-skill pass that picks the single carrier, and the real numbers from Will Nelson's own feed that motivate the cap.

## Personalization

Reads from `my-story/`:

- `narrative.md`: the funnel section ("what the posts must add up to") says what a post feeds next; the magnet must sit on that path, not beside it.
- `pillars.md`: the magnet map keys off the pillars. No pillars, no map.
- `icp.md`: the magnet must be built for buyers. A magnet that peers download and buyers ignore passes the earn test and still fails the funnel.
- `what-worked.md`: read past CTA posts' pull-through before choosing this window's carrier; write this window's CTA log here.

With an empty pack the skill still enforces the cap, the placement rules, the story-post ban, and the form tradeoff on any magnet the user supplies, but it cannot build the magnet map; it asks the user which pillar the magnet serves and proceeds with that answer, marked as unconfirmed.

## Examples

The earn test.

Bad: a post walking through why founder-led content drove a client's cost of acquisition down ends with "Enjoyed this? Subscribe to my weekly newsletter for more growth insights."
Better: the same post ends on its strongest line, then below it: "The CAC worksheet we used for this math is here: [link]."
Why: the newsletter is a different promise wearing the post's audience; the worksheet is the post, continued.

The story post.

Bad: a post about the meeting where five leaders gave five different answers to "what does this company do" ends with "I turned this into a framework. Comment ALIGN and I will send it."
Better: the post ends on the fifth answer landing, and carries nothing. The framework waits for a how-to post that actually promises it.
Why: the ask converts the story into a lead-in, and the reader feels the switch; hard rule 4 has no exceptions.

The form, weighed honestly.

Bad: "I'm giving this away because I love this community. Comment YES below and I will DM it to you (must be connected)."
Better: "The scorecard is at the link below. A link usually travels less far than a comment thread, and I am fine with that." Or, if the user chooses the gate after hearing the tradeoff: "If you want the scorecard, comment SCORECARD and I will send it. I am asking for the comment because comments carry the post further; that is the trade."
Why: the bad one is a gate dressed as generosity; both better versions make the mechanics honest, which is the only way a gate survives the bait ban.

The verdict.

Bad: "The CTA post got 14 reactions against our usual 80. The magnet is not working, drop it."
Better: "14 reactions, 9 keyword comments, 6 sends to ICP-fit founders, 2 replies turned into calls. Lowest reach of the month, best pull-through of the quarter. Keep the magnet, fix the hook."
Why: hard rule 8; a funnel CTA post is bought with reach and paid back in conversions, so reactions are the wrong ledger.

## Self-check

- Does the CTA continue the post's exact promise, in the reader's words, not just share a topic with it?
- Is this the only funnel CTA in the last 4 to 5 posts, counting the plan, not just what published?
- Does the post pass the delete test: fully working with the CTA removed?
- Is the last content line still the strongest beat, with the CTA below it as its own block?
- Is the carrier definitely not a story post?
- Does the pillar this post serves map to this magnet in the magnet map?
- If the form is a comment gate, was the reach-for-trust trade stated to the user in plain terms, and did they choose it?
- Does the magnet exist right now, finished, sendable today?
- Is the measurement plan logged in `what-worked.md` with conversions, not reactions, as the verdict?

## Related skills

- `linkedin-post` owns the ending. Its law, end on the strongest concrete beat with nothing performing after it, is upstream of this skill; the CTA is a block after the ending, never a replacement for it.
- `engagement-to-pipeline` handles what a CTA pulls in: keyword commenters and link clickers get tiered and routed there, never sprayed.
- `newsletter`: when the funnel's next step is the newsletter itself, that skill owns the issue; this skill only decides which post earns the pointer. Also the first stop when no magnet exists and one needs designing.
- `founder-pitch` knows which asset in the user's story is worth packaging; pair it with `newsletter` when working out what the first magnet could be.
- `what-worked` closes the loop: it weighs CTA posts by the pull-through this skill logged, so the next cycle learns from conversions.
- `anti-ai`: its engagement-bait scan runs on every CTA block before the user sees it.
