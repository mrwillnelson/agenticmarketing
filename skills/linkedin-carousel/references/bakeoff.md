# Bake-off: wall-of-text slides vs a slide plan

Read this when you want to see what the skill changes on a real input. Same source, two outputs: the baseline pastes paragraphs onto slides; the with-skill version runs the procedure and delivers a slide plan with a design notes column and a standalone caption.

## The source

Real material from Will Nelson's published LinkedIn posts. The numbers are his actual captured results; the five-step process is quoted from his own post.

From a post that pulled 17 reactions on the process behind a client result:

> 669,000 impressions in 28 days from founder content on LinkedIn
>
> we're just capturing stories of the company and getting the founder to share them in his voice
>
> here's our process:
> 1. Craft a great story and narrative
> 2. Create a process to capture stories inside and outside your company
> 3. Turn those story ideas into posts
> 4. Measure what works
> 5. Amplify your top performers with LinkedIn thought-leader ads

From a second post on the same system applied to another account:

> In the last 90 days, 64stories has grown an established CEO's LinkedIn account by 500% by doing something really simple.
>
> We share company milestones through the CEO's voice.
>
> If something starts to take off, they get boosted as Thought Leader Ads.
>
> All likes and engagements from the posts are scraped and sent to sales to nurture.

A numbered process with proof on both ends: this is a steppable framework, so it passes the format-fit gate. The same material as one claim plus one number would have been a text post.

## Baseline: without the skill

Six slides, each a pasted paragraph. The first three shown in full; the rest match.

Slide 1 (cover): "How 64stories Builds Founder-Led Content Engines That Actually Drive Results: Our Complete 5-Step Story Engineering Process Explained"

Slide 2: "The first and most important step is crafting a great story and narrative, because the secret to what we do is creating a narrative that aligns your content and, more importantly, your company, so that everyone from the CEO to the newest hire is rowing in the same direction toward the same target instead of going in circles."

Slide 3: "Next, you need to create a repeatable process to capture stories both inside and outside your company, because the raw material for great founder content already exists in your calls, your meetings, and your customer conversations, but without a capture process it evaporates before anyone writes it down, which is why this step matters."

Slides 4 through 6 continue at 50 to 65 words each, and slide 6 ends: "Want this for your company? Follow Will Nelson for everything founder-led growth, and get in touch!"

What went wrong: the cover is a 19-word report title that dies at thumbnail size. Six slides means the idea got compressed instead of stepped. Every slide is a wall at arm's length. The last slide is an ad, so the deck ends without landing anything. There is no caption and no design notes, so whoever builds the visuals starts from scratch.

## With the skill

Step trace, kept short:

1. Format fit: five numbered steps plus proof on both ends. It steps. Proceed.
2. The promise: by the last slide the reader knows the exact five-step system behind 669,000 impressions in 28 days.
3. Cover via `hooks`: the real number is the highest-voltage signal. One claim, ten words, reads as a thumbnail.
4. Beat map: cover, setup, five steps, second proof, payoff. Nine slides, inside the 8 to 12 band.

The slide plan:

| # | Slide text | Visual beat |
|---|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | 669,000 impressions in 28 days. From one founder's voice. | The number huge, nothing else on the slide |
| 2 | No ads first. No ghostwritten hot takes. We captured stories that already existed inside the company. He shared them in his voice. | Plain statement card, sets up the list |
| 3 | Step 1. Craft a great story and narrative. One narrative that aligns the content and, more importantly, the company. | Step counter "1/5", the word "one" underlined |
| 4 | Step 2. Build a capture process. Stories live inside and outside the company. Without a process, they evaporate before anyone writes them down. | Step counter "2/5", funnel of raw sources |
| 5 | Step 3. Turn story ideas into posts. In the founder's voice, not the brand's. | Step counter "3/5", before/after voice swap |
| 6 | Step 4. Measure what works. Publish, then wait. Most posts are experiments. A few are signals. | Step counter "4/5", one bar spiking above flat bars |
| 7 | Step 5. Amplify the winners as thought-leader ads. Engagement gets scraped and sent to sales to nurture. | Step counter "5/5", post-to-pipeline arrow |
| 8 | Same system, different account: an established CEO's LinkedIn grown 500% in 90 days. | The second number huge, mirroring slide 1 |
| 9 | The secret was never writing tricks. It was everyone rowing toward the same target. Craft. Capture. Create. Measure. Amplify. | The five words stacked as the full system |

Every slide is one idea, 25 words or fewer. Slide 9 lands the cover's promise and no CTA slide follows it.

The caption:

> 669,000 impressions in 28 days from founder content on LinkedIn.
>
> We didn't post more. We captured stories that already existed inside the company and got the founder to share them in his voice.
>
> Five steps: craft the story, build the capture process, turn ideas into posts, measure what works, amplify the winners as thought-leader ads.
>
> The same system grew an established CEO's account 500% in 90 days.
>
> The writing was never the hard part.

Hook first, stands alone, no swipe language, ends a beat early. Seventy-nine words, on Will's measured median.

## Why the slide plan beats the wall of text

1. The cover is a hook, not a title. Ten words and one open question at thumbnail size, against a 19-word label that needs a magnifying glass. The cover carries the whole stop-the-scroll burden and the baseline spent it on throat-clearing.
2. One idea per slide makes the swipe the unit of pacing. Nine slides, one beat each, so every swipe pays off. The baseline's six paragraphs force the reader to do the chunking the author skipped.
3. The 25-word ceiling is checked, not vibed. The baseline's slide 3 runs 58 words; at arm's length that is a gray rectangle.
4. The payoff lands the promise. Slide 9 resolves the cover's question with the system compressed to five words. The baseline ends on "get in touch!", which is an ad where the payoff should be.
5. The caption earns its own readers. A feed reader who never opens the document still gets a complete post with both real numbers. The baseline had no caption at all.
6. The design notes column makes the deck buildable. Nine visual beats, one line each; the designer never reads the source posts. The baseline hands over six paragraphs and a shrug.
7. Every number is Will's real published result: 669,000 impressions in 28 days, 500% in 90 days. Nothing was invented to fill a slide.
