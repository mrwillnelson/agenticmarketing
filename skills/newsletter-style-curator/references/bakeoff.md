# Bake-off: link roundup vs curation with a spine

One week's reading shipped two ways. All four picks below are invented for this exercise, and so are their authors and numbers. The curator and the receipts are real: Will Nelson, founder of 64stories.com, curating for an `icp.md` of B2B founders and CEOs plus marketing leaders at founder-led companies.

## The candidate picks (synthetic)

| # | Source type | The piece | Why it surfaced |
|---|-------------|-----------|-----------------|
| 1 | Industry benchmark report | "Company page reach fell 61% year over year; personal exec accounts grew 3x" from an invented analytics newsletter | Everyone in the ICP's feed is screenshotting one chart from it |
| 2 | Personal blog essay | A seed-stage founder's honest twelve-month log of writing every post himself, including the months nothing worked | Found via a comment thread logged by `comment-strategy` on Tuesday |
| 3 | Launch-site product post | An AI tool that scrapes a company website and generates a month of "founder voice" posts automatically | Three separate ICP contacts shared it this week, all approvingly |
| 4 | Podcast transcript | A COO interview on discovering that sales, product, and the CEO were each telling a different company story | Sits squarely on the narrative pillar in `pillars.md` |

## Baseline: the link roundup

> Happy Friday! Here are four things worth your time this week:
>
> 1. Wild data on company pages vs personal accounts. The chart says it all. [link]
> 2. Loved this founder's honesty about writing his own posts for a year. [link]
> 3. A new AI tool that writes founder content from your website. Cool demo. [link]
> 4. Great podcast on company storytelling. Worth a listen on your commute. [link]
>
> That's all for this week! Reply with anything I missed.

What happens: run the spine test and delete the links. Nothing remains except "happy Friday." Every pick got applause a feed could have generated, pick 3 shipped as an implied endorsement of a tool the curator has never run, and nobody replies because there is nothing to agree or argue with. The issue transferred four clicks and zero judgment.

## With the skill: the spined issue

The spine, stated in the open before any link:

> Everything worth reading this week points at one shift: the market stopped grading the content and started grading whether the named author actually holds the view. Four exhibits, one of which I think is wrong.

Pick 1, the benchmark report. Commentary adds the flaw the screenshots skip:

> The chart everyone is sharing measures reach, not trust, and part of that 61% drop is platforms throttling company pages on purpose. Read it as "distribution moved to people," not "your founder is suddenly a genius." If you are pre-product with no story to distribute yet, this report is not your permission slip.

Pick 2, the founder's twelve-month log. Commentary adds firsthand experience, spending a real receipt:

> His slowest months match what I see when founders outsource the thinking along with the typing. When 64stories grew an established CEO's account 500% in 90 days (my post of 2026-03-12), the CEO still shaped every post with us on a call every two weeks. The essay's real lesson is not "write it yourself." It is that the hour of deciding what is true enough to say cannot be delegated, whoever does the typing.

Pick 3, the scrape-to-posts tool. The disagreement, on the record:

> This is the one I think is wrong, and three of you sent it to me approvingly, so let me earn the disagreement. A tool that generates a founder's views from a website scrape optimizes exactly the thing the market started auditing. As I wrote in the piece that has done best for me this year (2026-02-26, 141 reactions): "Your story is your strategy, made legible." A scraper cannot make your decisions legible when the decisions have not been made; it can only make the absence fluent.

Pick 4, the COO interview. Commentary names who should act and where the piece stops short:

> The three-stories problem she describes is the private version of the public shift above; the audit happens inside the company first. The interview stops at diagnosis, so here is the next step it skips: get the five leaders in a room and make them each explain the company in two minutes, recorded. If you are a marketing leader who suspects drift, that recording is your week's best content brief.

The close, two sentences tying back, then the earned ending:

> Four different rooms, one audit: does the person with their name on it actually hold the view? The report shows where attention went, the essay and the tool show the two ways to respond, and the COO shows what it costs internally when nobody holds the pen. Which of the four would you push back on? Reply with the number; the best pushback runs in next week's issue with credit.

## What changed, annotated

- The spine appears before the first link and survives the spine test. Delete all four links and the issue still argues that authorship is being audited, with a receipt-backed position on what to do about it.
- Every pick's commentary adds what the source does not say: the report gets its unmentioned measurement flaw plus a named reader who should ignore it, the essay gets firsthand experience, the tool gets the disagreement, the interview gets the missing next step.
- The disagreement is placed third, not first: by then the spine has earned the standing to push against a pick three readers personally shared. Disagreeing in the open is also what makes the praise elsewhere worth anything.
- Source diversity holds: a report, a personal blog, a launch site, a podcast. Four picks, four watering holes.
- The receipts are real and exact where the picks are invented: 500% in 90 days from Will's published post of 2026-03-12, and "Your story is your strategy, made legible" quoted verbatim from his 2026-02-26 post (141 reactions, 35 comments). Invented evidence is labeled; real evidence is dated.
- Attribution stays generous even for invented sources: each pick is credited to its author and format, and the quote in pick 3 is Will quoting himself, marked as such.
- The ending is the parent skill's discipline doing its job: a question only this issue earned, plus a concrete tease that feeds next week.
