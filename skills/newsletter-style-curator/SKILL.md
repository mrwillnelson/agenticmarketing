# Newsletter-Style Curator

Curation is an argument made with other people's work. The spine is a thesis this week's picks all point at; the picks are the evidence; the commentary is what the reader pays for. This skill layers on `newsletter`, so everything there still binds: the two-part subject and preview hook, one reader, a reply-worthy ending, and the `anti-ai` and `draft-qa` gates. Nothing below restates those; it adds the curation craft on top.

## Hard rules

1. The spine comes first. One sentence naming the shift this week's picks all point at, stated in the open before any link appears. Picks that do not point at it are not this issue's picks; bank them.
2. 3 to 5 picks maximum. Each pick earns its slot with commentary that adds something the source does not say: your experience with it, the flaw nobody mentions, or who specifically should ignore it. A pick carrying only "great read" gets cut, not padded.
3. Commentary is the product; the link is the excuse. Apply the spine test before shipping: delete every link and read what remains. If the issue no longer says anything, it is a feed, not a letter.
4. Disagree with at least one pick per issue, on the record and with a reason. All-praise curation is a repost feed with extra steps.
5. Source diversity: never three picks from the same watering hole. Same publication, same platform, or the same author's orbit all count as one hole.
6. Attribution is generous and exact. Name the author the way they name themselves, link the original (not an aggregator), and quote verbatim or not at all.

## Procedure

1. Gather candidates from the week's reading. Start with the `comment-strategy` log if one exists; it is a week of pre-scored threads in the ICP's world. Add anything saved from `listening-sources.md` haunts. Aim for 8 to 12 candidates before cutting.
2. Find the spine. Sort candidates into loose piles and look for the shift 3 to 5 of them point at from different angles. Name it in one sentence a reader could repeat. If two spines emerge, pick one and bank the other pile for a future issue. If no spine emerges, this is not curation week; hand back to `newsletter` for a one-lesson letter instead.
3. Cut to picks with the slot test. For each survivor, write the one thing you would add that the source does not say. No addition, no slot. Then run the diversity check against rule 5 and confirm at least one pick is there to be argued with, not endorsed.
4. Write the commentary, 2 to 5 sentences per pick. Lead with your addition, not a summary; the reader can get the summary from the link. Each pick's commentary does at least one of: firsthand experience with the idea, the flaw the applause is skipping, or a named reader who should skip it ("if you have no sales team yet, ignore this one").
5. Order the picks so the argument builds. Open with the pick that states the shift most plainly, put the disagreement where it sharpens rather than opens, and let the last pick land closest to the reader's next decision.
6. Close in two sentences that tie the picks back to the spine without repeating it word for word. Then apply the `newsletter` ending discipline: a question this issue earned, a specific ask, or a concrete tease.
7. Subject and preview last, per the parent skill, with the `hooks` fold checks. The subject sells the spine, never "5 links for your weekend."
8. Run the spine test one final time, then gate through `anti-ai` and `draft-qa`.

## Personalization

Reads from `my-story/`:

- `narrative.md`: the spine should sit inside the running story. A spine that cuts against the narrative can still ship, but only on purpose and saying so.
- `pillars.md`: picks cluster under pillars. A spine that touches no pillar is somebody else's newsletter; curate for the positioning, not the timeline.
- `icp.md`: curate for the reader you want, not the feed you have. "Who should ignore this" is written to the ICP by name of role.
- `listening-sources.md`: where candidates come from, and the map of watering holes the diversity rule counts against.

With an empty pack the skill still works: the spine comes from whatever the candidates share, and the commentary leans on general judgment. The output will be a coherent issue aimed at nobody in particular. Filling `icp.md` and `pillars.md` is what turns it into curation for a specific reader.

## Examples

Bad pick commentary: "This piece on founder brand is fantastic. Highly recommend giving it a read!"
Better: "The piece says post daily. We ran daily cadence for six weeks and watched reply quality drop while impressions climbed; the advice holds only if someone is actually working the replies. No one on comments? Skip this one and post twice a week instead."
Why: the bad one is applause a feed algorithm could have written; the better one adds firsthand experience, the unmentioned flaw, and a named reader who should ignore it.

Bad open: "Happy Friday! Here are five things I enjoyed reading this week:"
Better: "Three of the best things I read this week say the same quiet thing: buyers stopped grading the content and started grading the author. Here they are, and where I think one of them is wrong."
Why: the bad one promises a feed; the better one states the spine in the open and promises a disagreement, which is a reason to read past the first link.

Bad attribution: "There's a thread going around about agency pricing that makes some solid points."
Better: "From a bootstrapped agency founder's public teardown of her own pricing page (linked below, her numbers quoted as she published them):"
Why: the bad one strips the author out of their own work; the better one credits exactly, links the original, and keeps the quote verbatim.

## Self-check

- Can the spine be stated in one sentence, and does it appear before the first link?
- Are there 3 to 5 picks, and does each one's commentary say something the source does not?
- Is at least one pick disagreed with, with a reason a reader could push back on?
- Do any three picks share a watering hole?
- Is every author named exactly and linked at the original?
- Delete the links: does the issue still say something?
- Did the draft pass every check the parent `newsletter` skill imposes, including the fold checks and both gates?

Read `references/bakeoff.md` to see the same four picks shipped as a baseline link roundup and as a spined issue, with the differences annotated.
