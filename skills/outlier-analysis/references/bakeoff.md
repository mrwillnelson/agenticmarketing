# Bake-off: baseline vs outlier-analysis on three real posts

The pasted input below is three real LinkedIn posts by Will Nelson, Founder of 64stories.com, used with his real engagement numbers because his material is the only real material this repo uses. Three posts is below the skill's 10-post minimum, so this run is illustrative: with one post per engine, rule 5 keeps every entry marked as an untested lead. Fit is assessed against Will's measured voice fingerprint from his 33-post gold set: median 79 words per post (range genuinely unbounded), median 18 words per sentence, long sentences (29%) outnumbering short (25%), 0.6 sentences per paragraph, no rhetorical questions, no hashtags, endings that stop a beat early.

## The baseline take (no skill)

"All three posts worked because they are relatable and punchy. Will writes with authenticity and confidence, opens with strong hooks, and keeps paragraphs short so the posts are easy to scan. To get similar results: be authentic, hook readers early, keep it punchy, and use short paragraphs."

Every sentence of that is unfalsifiable, none of it cites a line, and "punchy" is factually wrong for two of the three posts. Nothing in it tells a writer what to build next.

## The with-skill analysis

### Card A: the belief teardown

- Hook pattern: Aha. "Ask a founder about storytelling and they'll point you to their marketing team." followed by "That tells you everything." Open question: what does it tell you? The hook indicts a behavior the reader has done this week without saying why it is wrong.
- Structural engine: teardown of a common belief. Beats: the common behavior, the indictment, the hidden equivalence ("The two problems are the same problem."), the observed pattern, the mechanism ("The gaps in your narrative reveal the gaps in your strategy."), stakes at scale, the reframe landing ("Your story is your strategy, made legible.").
- Rhythm profile: one-line hook, one-line indictment, then reasoning paragraphs of 2 to 4 sentences averaging near 18 words. Shortest sentence sits second, as the turn.
- Proof type: story of a repeated pattern ("I've seen this dozens of times."). No numbers.
- Driver: recognition. The reader is the founder who pointed to the marketing team.
- Flags: none. The argument works from any account; nothing depends on the author's name.

### Card B: the diagnostic

- Hook pattern: Aha. "The fastest way to tell a company is drifting." Open question: what is the test? A promised diagnostic the reader can run today.
- Structural engine: not in the standard five; named by its job, the diagnostic. Beats: symptom, the test ("Ask five leaders to explain what the company does."), the predicted result, the consequence ("every roadmap debate becomes an argument."), pointer to depth.
- Rhythm profile: five one-line paragraphs, sentences of 5 to 11 words, markedly staccato. Shortest sentence is the payoff line.
- Proof type: demonstration. The reader can verify it without trusting the author.
- Driver: fear of a hidden problem, with recognition underneath.
- Flags: none. The comment count relative to length suggests the test itself did the work.

### Card C: the receipts-led process reveal

- Hook pattern: Hell yeah with an Aha underneath. "669,000 impressions in 28 days from founder content on LinkedIn". Open question: how? A bare number with no adjectives.
- Structural engine: receipts-led. Beats: the result, the plain-spoken method ("we're just capturing stories of the company"), the one secret, named precedents, a numbered process, an open invitation.
- Rhythm profile: lowercase one-line paragraphs, one precedent list, one numbered list, no landing flourish.
- Proof type: numbers, plus demonstration in the process steps.
- Driver: permission. "we're just capturing stories" says the result is attainable, not genius.
- Flags: checked for authority dependence and passed. The load-bearing line is the number, not the name; an unknown account with the same real number keeps the engine. Copyable: the beat order and the bare-number hook pattern. Not copyable: the 669,000, the client story, and 64stories' credibility. The entry's risk line carries that.

### Fit against the measured fingerprint

- Belief teardown: good fit. Reasoning-bearing sentences near 18 words match the median exactly, and the fingerprint says sentences carry reasoning. The 195-word length sits inside a genuinely unbounded range.
- Diagnostic: workable with adaptation. The one-line paragraphs match 0.6 sentences per paragraph, but the 5-to-11-word staccato runs against a voice where long sentences outnumber short. Usable as a short teaser format, not a default register.
- Receipts-led: good fit when, and only when, a real number exists. The no-flourish ending matches endings that stop a beat early.

All three entries enter `my-story/format-library.md` marked untested lead, 1 post each, below the 3-post threshold.

## What changed, in one paragraph

The baseline produced adjectives; the skill produced machinery. "Relatable and punchy" cannot be executed, cannot be falsified, and is wrong about rhythm for the two posts that win on 18-word reasoning sentences rather than punch. The with-skill pass pins every claim to a quoted line, so each conclusion can be checked against the post it came from; it separates what transfers (the teardown's beat order, the diagnostic's test-then-consequence shape, the bare-number hook) from what does not (the 669,000, the client story, Will's name); it catches that the strongest-looking staccato rhythm is the worst fit for this voice; and it hands `format-remix` skeletons with fit calls attached, so the next draft starts from structure the user can actually carry instead of an imitation of someone else's register.
