# Contrarian style bake-off

Read this when you want to see the full delta the style produces on one belief: the same takedown drafted as baseline rage-bait and then through the skill, with every step traced.

The belief being cracked is generic startup advice, held by no named person. The founder material is real: Will Nelson, Founder of 64stories.com, quoted verbatim from his own published LinkedIn posts. Nothing in the receipts is invented for this document.

## The belief

"If people don't understand what your company does, it's a messaging problem. Hire a better writer."

Worth cracking because it is common, it is defensible, and Will has watched it fail from inside real engagements. That combination is exactly what the standing check looks for.

## Baseline output (no skill)

> Marketing experts are scamming founders and I'm done staying quiet about it.
>
> Everyone tells you the same thing. Fix your messaging. Hire a copywriter. Redo the website. Wrong.
>
> Agencies will happily take ten grand a month to rearrange words while your company dies. They know the words were never the problem. They just don't care.
>
> Messaging is dead. Strategy is everything. It always was.
>
> Unfollow anyone who tells you different. And if you're one of the smart founders who already knew this, smash that follow button and comment STRATEGY below. Let that sink in.

What went wrong, line by line:

- The opening attacks people, not an idea. "Marketing experts are scamming founders" has faces on it, and rule 3 bans every one of them.
- The belief is never stated fairly. "Fix your messaging" is flattened into a scam, so anyone who has ever benefited from a good writer, which is most of the audience, stops trusting the speaker by second four.
- No receipt anywhere. Not one number, not one moment, nothing the speaker witnessed. The take is pure attitude, which the `anti-patterns.md` pack bans by name: fake contrarianism.
- "Messaging is dead" is the all-or-nothing tell. The common take is right most of the time and the script pretends otherwise, so it collapses on contact with one counterexample in the comments.
- No reframe and no Monday move. A viewer who agrees with every word still leaves with nothing to do.
- "Let that sink in" and the follow-and-comment closer are banned phrases and engagement bait, and the parent's hard-cut rule bans everything after the payoff anyway.

## With the skill

Standing check (step 1), receipts pulled from Will's published posts:

- "I've seen this dozens of times. A founder struggles to tell their story. They blame it on being 'too technical.' But dig deeper and the real issue surfaces. They haven't made the hard decisions about what they are and what they're not." (post 2026-02-26, 141 reactions)
- "Ask five leaders to explain what the company does. You'll hear five different stories." (post 2026-03-04)
- The reframe already exists in his own words: "Your story is your strategy, made legible." (post 2026-02-26)

Standing confirmed: repeated firsthand observation, a diagnostic he actually uses, and a receipt-backed model. The style is available.

Steelman test (step 2): "When the strategy is clear and the page still confuses people, a good writer fixes it in a week." A copywriter would nod at that. Pass.

Score (step 3): the common take is right most of the time; wording problems are real and writers solve them. The both-things-true line goes in the first beat, and the crack names the specific case where the advice breaks: when the leaders themselves tell different stories.

Crack (step 4): one receipt, the "dozens of times" pattern, with the five-leaders moment held for the Monday move so each beat carries one thing.

Reframe (step 5): "Your story is your strategy, made legible." Usability test: a viewer can act on it, because the Monday move operationalizes it without Will in the room.

The script, handed to `short-form-script` (steps 6 and 7): 60 to 90 second argument band, about 175 spoken words, hook inside 3 seconds, crack at the first dip, reframe at the second, hard cut on the move.

| Spoken | Visual |
|---|---|
| A better writer won't fix your confusing story. | Overlay: "the advice that wastes a quarter". Talking head, tight. |
| The advice sounds right. If customers don't get what you do, sharpen the copy, hire a stronger writer. And most of the time that's true. When the strategy is clear and the page still confuses people, a good writer fixes it in a week. | Hold on camera. Beat of agreement before the turn. |
| But I've seen this dozens of times. A founder struggles to tell their story. They blame it on being too technical. Dig deeper and the real issue surfaces. They haven't made the hard decisions about what they are and what they're not. The words can't be clear because the decision was never made. | Cut closer at "dozens of times". |
| Your story is your strategy, made legible. If the story won't hold together, the strategy won't either. | Overlay: "story = strategy, made legible". |
| So before you hire anyone, run one test. Ask five leaders at your company to explain what it does. If you hear five different stories, the copy was never the problem. Get in a room and make the call the copy has been waiting on. | Hold on camera. Hard cut on the last word. |

Heat pass (step 8): no hedges, no rage markers, no person or company attacked; the fault lives in an undecided strategy, not in writers or agencies. Notable because 64stories sells narrative work and the script still concedes the case where a writer alone is the right hire.

Why this passes where the baseline failed:

- The belief gets its fair statement, so the crack lands on something the viewer actually holds.
- The take is earned: the pattern is Will's repeated firsthand observation, quoted from his own published material, not attitude manufactured for the camera.
- Both things are true on camera. The script says when the common advice works, then names the exact condition where it breaks, so the comments have nothing cheap to puncture.
- The reframe is his own sentence and the Monday move makes it runnable this week: a five-person test any founder can do before spending a dollar.
- Voice holds: contractions, one long reasoning sentence in the middle beats, no rhetorical questions, and the ending stops on the move with nothing after it.
