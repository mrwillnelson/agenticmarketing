# What to Say Bakeoff

Same evidence. Two ways of responding.

The point of this comparison is to show the difference between plausible content ideation and an evidence-backed editorial decision.

## Input

A fictional B2B infrastructure company provides this bundle:

- Four sales calls. Three prospects say rollout risk and migration time are bigger concerns than feature gaps.
- Two customer calls. Both customers praise the implementation team's migration checklist and staged rollout process.
- One support summary. The largest cluster of pre-purchase questions is about migration sequencing.
- A competitor launches an AI assistant and receives a burst of industry attention.
- Keyword research shows high volume around `AI infrastructure automation` and much smaller volume around migration planning.
- The company has no proprietary AI model or unusual AI feature. It does have implementation data from 120 migrations.

Question:

`What should we be saying next?`

## Without What to Say

A generic content response might produce:

1. Five ways AI is changing infrastructure automation
2. The future of AI-powered infrastructure
3. How to migrate infrastructure without downtime
4. Our take on the competitor's AI launch
5. Infrastructure automation trends to watch
6. Migration checklist for engineering teams
7. Why implementation matters as much as features
8. AI vs traditional infrastructure workflows
9. Customer success story about a migration
10. A guide to choosing an infrastructure platform

Why this is weak:

- It treats every signal as a topic generator.
- The competitor launch gets disproportionate weight because it is vivid and current.
- Search volume becomes a reason to talk about AI even though the company has little authority there.
- The repeated migration signal is one item in a list instead of the decision.
- The creator still has to decide what deserves to exist.

## With What to Say

### Decision

The company should say: **the real infrastructure buying risk is not missing one more feature. It is whether the team can migrate safely and predictably, and buyers should evaluate vendors on the migration system as seriously as the product.**

### Why this wins

**Buyer pull: strong**

Three of four sales calls independently make rollout risk a larger objection than feature gaps. Support questions point to the same concern.

**Right to say: strong**

The company has a repeatable migration process, customers explicitly value it, and it has data from 120 migrations.

**Why now: strong**

The competitor AI launch is useful context because it is pulling the market back toward feature comparison. That creates a moment to reframe how buyers should evaluate the category.

**Proof: strong**

The company can show its staged rollout process, checklist, customer evidence, and migration data.

**Distinctiveness: strong**

Most category attention is moving toward AI features. A buyer-risk message backed by implementation evidence gives the company a credible different axis.

### Search decision

Do not chase `AI infrastructure automation` simply because it has more volume. Use search research to discover how buyers phrase migration risk, implementation planning, downtime, switching, and rollout questions. The current evidence supports the buyer problem more strongly than the high-volume AI topic.

### How to say it

Lead with the buying mistake: teams compare feature matrices and underweight migration risk. Show what the company has learned across 120 migrations. Turn the migration checklist into proof, not a generic gated asset.

The competitor launch can appear as timing context. It should not become the subject of the piece.

### What not to claim

Do not claim the company's migration process guarantees zero downtime unless the data supports that statement. Do not claim buyers do not care about AI. The evidence only shows that rollout risk is currently more important in these sales conversations.

## What changed

Without the skill, the creator receives ten plausible things to make.

With the skill, the creator receives one decision:

> **Own migration risk as the category conversation, and use the AI news as timing rather than the thesis.**

That is the job this skill must keep proving in real runs.
