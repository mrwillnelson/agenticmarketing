# The press kit: one afternoon of owned foundation

A pitch that gets a "maybe" dies in the back-and-forth: "do you have a bio?", "can you send a headshot?", "what does the company actually do?". Each round trip is a day of cooling interest. The press kit answers all of it before anyone asks, which is why the pitch links it in the signature. Build it once, in one afternoon, before the first pitch goes out.

## The checklist

Work through these in order. Everything on this list is content you already have or can write in under an hour.

1. **One-paragraph company description, in plain language.** What you do, for whom, and the one number or fact that makes it credible. No category jargon, no vision statement. The test: a podcast host should be able to read it aloud as your introduction without editing it.
2. **Founder bio, short and long.** The short one is two sentences for a newsletter blurb or episode description. The long one is a paragraph for show notes and op-ed bylines: what you built, what you did before, and the one credential that earns your opinion on your topic. Write both in third person; that is how they get used.
3. **Headshots and logo files at usable resolutions.** At least one headshot at 1500px or wider on the short side, one square crop, and the logo as both a transparent PNG and an SVG, on light and dark backgrounds. Low-resolution assets are the most common reason a confirmed feature ships without your face or your mark.
4. **Real numbers you are willing to cite.** Three to five, each one true, current, and pre-approved for print: customers, growth, usage, revenue if you share it, or content performance. Deciding what is quotable now means never stalling a journalist on "let me check if I can say that". Update these quarterly; a stale number quoted in print is worse than none.
5. **Two or three approved founder quotes.** Written in the founder's actual voice, each making one point from the narrative. Newsletter writers on deadline will lift these verbatim, which is the goal: you wrote your own coverage. No hype vocabulary; a quote that says "revolutionary" will be the one line they cut.
6. **Contact route.** One email that a human answers within a business day, and a note on response time. If a booking link exists for podcast scheduling, include it here, not in the pitch.

## Where it lives

Two options, both fine:

- **A /press page on your site.** Best if the site is easy to update. One page, all six items, assets as direct downloads.
- **A public Notion doc.** Zero engineering, ten minutes to publish, and just as linkable. Title it "[Company] press kit" so it reads as intentional, not improvised.

Either way: one URL, no login, no form, no "request access". Link it under the signature of every pitch, like this:

> Will Nelson
> Founder, 64stories.com
> Press kit: [one URL]

The kit is what converts a "maybe" into a "yes" without another email. When someone opens it and finds the description, the bio, the numbers, and the images all ready, you have just become the easiest feature on their calendar this month.

## Maintenance

- Recheck the numbers quarterly. Remove anything you can no longer stand behind.
- After each piece of coverage, add a short "As featured in" line with links. Proof of past coverage lowers the perceived risk of being first.
- When the narrative shifts, rewrite the description and quotes the same week. The kit drifting from the current story is how old positioning keeps getting printed.
