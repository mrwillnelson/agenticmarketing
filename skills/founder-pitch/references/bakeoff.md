# Bake-off: the blast vs the pitch

One target, two pitches. The sender is real: Will Nelson, Founder of 64stories.com, and the 669,000-impressions number comes from his published LinkedIn post of 2026-03-03. The target is synthetic: **The Content Ledger, a 6,200-subscriber weekly newsletter about B2B content and demand gen, written solo by "Maya Lindqvist", is invented for this exercise**, as is her issue title referenced below. In real use, the referenced piece must be one you actually read; that is hard rule 2.

Target scoring, for the record: audience-ICP overlap high (B2B founders and marketers read her), beat fit high (founder-led content is her recurring theme), accessibility high (solo writer, answers her own inbox), timing high (covered the topic this month). This is exactly the target the skill says to weight over tier-1 press.

## Baseline: the 280-word blast

> Subject: Partnership opportunity with an exciting startup

"Partnership opportunity" is the second most deleted subject line in her inbox, right after "quick question". It carries no story, no number, no reason to open.

> Hi Maya, I hope this email finds you well! I've been a longtime fan of The Content Ledger and love everything you write. Your insights on B2B content are truly second to none, and I share your newsletter with my whole team every single week.

Fake familiarity, and she can tell. "Love everything you write" references nothing, so it verifies nothing. Every recipient has learned that unfalsifiable flattery means the sender read zero issues.

> I'm reaching out because I think there's some incredible synergy between what you cover and what we're building. I'm the founder of 64stories, a revolutionary AI-powered platform that is completely transforming how B2B companies approach storytelling and founder-led content.

"Synergy", "revolutionary", "transforming": three banned words in two sentences, and still no story. Note what the paragraph is about: the sender. Her readers have not appeared yet.

> Our cutting-edge approach combines game-changing narrative frameworks with data-driven insights to help founders unlock exponential growth on LinkedIn and beyond.

Four more hype terms and zero verifiable claims. One real number would beat this entire paragraph.

> We've been getting some amazing results for our clients, and our customers consistently see massive boosts in engagement, followers, and inbound leads within just a few weeks of onboarding. I truly believe your audience would love to hear about the disruptive work we're doing in this space. We're on a mission to change the way the world thinks about content, and we're just getting started.

"Amazing results" and "massive boosts" with no number are unsupported claims; the sender clearly has data and is choosing adjectives instead. The one mention of her audience is a guess about what they would love, offered with no evidence.

> I'd love to explore a few ways we could work together. First, would you be open to featuring 64stories in an upcoming issue of The Content Ledger? I've attached our press release, a company one-pager, and a deck with more details. Second, we're also launching a podcast soon and would be honored to have you as one of our first guests. And third, if it's easier, maybe we could just hop on a quick 30-minute call sometime in the next week or two to explore where there might be a fit?

Three asks and three attachments in one paragraph. Three asks means she has to do the thinking, so she does none. Attachments on a cold first touch signal mass mail and trip spam filters.

> Looking forward to hearing from you! I know your time is valuable, so I promise it will be worth it.

The claim of valuing her time arrives at word 280 of an email that asked for a feature, a guest slot, and a call. The whole message is about the sender; nothing in it serves her readers. It gets archived unanswered, and the name is now marked as noise.

## With the skill: the 136-word pitch

> Subject: 669k impressions in 28 days from one founder's LinkedIn

A specific stat that is itself the story. It survives the inbox preview alone, and it promises exactly what the email delivers.

> Maya,

No "hope this finds you well". Writers who answer their own inbox read that as respect for their time.

> Your issue on why founder posts outperform brand accounts argued the gap is trust. I got a result that backs you up, with a wrinkle your readers might find useful.

Proof of familiarity that is falsifiable: it names a specific issue and engages its actual argument. In this exercise the issue is synthetic; in real use, only reference a piece you read. "A wrinkle" also signals this is not pure agreement, which is what makes it worth an issue.

> A 64stories client hit 669,000 impressions in 28 days from founder content on LinkedIn, and the number keeps climbing, pulling acquisition cost down with it. The mechanics are boring: we capture stories already inside the company and the founder shares them in his voice.

The data-story angle: one real number from the sender's own work, sourced from a published post, then an honest deflation of the mechanics. "Boring" does more credibility work than "revolutionary" ever could.

> The wrinkle: the driver was not posting tactics. It was getting the whole company behind one narrative, the same move Salesforce made with The End of Software, so the content and the company row in the same direction.

The implication that serves her audience: a transferable lesson, not a product feature. Her readers can use this whether or not they ever hear of 64stories.

> Would a short breakdown of that 28-day run, with post-level numbers, work for a future issue? Yes or no is a fine answer.

One ask, answerable in ten seconds, with the pressure explicitly removed. "Post-level numbers" tells her the piece will have receipts, not adjectives.

> Will Nelson
> Founder, 64stories.com
> Press kit: 64stories.com/press

No attachments. The press kit link means a "maybe" can become a "yes" without a single clarifying email: bio, headshot, description, and approved numbers are one click away.

## What changed, in five lines

- 280 words became 136, and the shorter one contains more verifiable information.
- Seven hype terms became zero; one real number replaced all of them.
- Unfalsifiable flattery became a falsifiable reference to a specific piece and its argument.
- Three asks and three attachments became one yes-or-no question and one link.
- The baseline serves the sender; the pitch serves her readers first, which is the only reason it also ends up serving the sender.

If this pitch gets no reply: one follow-up in 5 to 7 days with one new fact (for example, the account crossing 1M impressions), then silence, and the outcome gets logged in `my-story/what-worked.md` under data-story angle, niche newsletter target.
