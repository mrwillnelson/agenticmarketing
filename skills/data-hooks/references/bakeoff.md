# Data hooks bake-off

Read this when you want to see the full delta the skill produces on one data-backed idea, or when calibrating the number-versus-claim fork against a real case.

The idea and every number in it are real: Will Nelson, Founder of 64stories.com, quoted verbatim from his own published LinkedIn material. Nothing here is synthetic.

## The idea

A new 64stories client's founder content reached 669,000 impressions in 28 days, and the impressions were still climbing at time of writing. The post's job: show founders what a story-first content program produces, and open the "how" question the rest of the post answers.

## Baseline output (no skill): the vague-scale opener

> We're seeing some massive growth for one of our newest clients at 64stories.
>
> Founder-led content is absolutely exploding right now, and the results speak for themselves.
>
> Our client's impressions have skyrocketed over the last month, driving huge visibility for the brand.
>
> Here's how we did it.

What went wrong, line by line:

- "Massive growth" is a claim wearing a costume. The source has a real number, so every vague scale word here is a downgrade the writer chose.
- Line one spends the fold on the company name and a feeling. The reader learns 64stories is pleased, which is not information.
- "The results speak for themselves" while withholding the results. The one thing that could speak is missing.
- "Skyrocketed", "exploding", "huge": three banned scale words, each asking for trust the number would have earned outright.
- "Here's how we did it" promises a reveal before creating any reason to want one. There is no curiosity gap because there is nothing specific to be curious about.

## With the skill: the naked-number opener

Procedure trace:

- Inventory (step 1): 669,000 impressions, 28 days, provenance is the client's own analytics behind a post Will published himself. One number, two checkable units. Passes the provenance law unmodified: not rounded up, not extrapolated past the 28 measured days even though impressions were still climbing.
- Surprise test (step 2): the founder audience's model of a good organic month is low five figures of impressions. 669,000 violates that model on its own. No adjective required, which is the tell that the number should lead.
- Fork (step 3): number-led. The mechanism (story capture in the founder's voice) is strong but ordinary-sounding in one line; the number is the model-breaker.
- One-number check: "in 28 days" is the timeframe of the measurement, part of the unit, not a second number. Nothing else from the program (post counts, follower counts) is allowed into line one.
- So-what gate: the implication for a founder reader is direct. This channel can carry acquisition weight, which means founder content is worth their calendar time.

The opener Will actually published, verbatim:

> 669,000 impressions in 28 days from founder content on LinkedIn
>
> these are the results we're seeing from a new client at 64stories, and the impressions keep climbing – driving cost of acquisition lower and lower

Why this passes where the baseline failed:

- Line one is naked: the number and its units before any framing, any feeling, any company name. The reader reacts to the number before knowing whose it is.
- Line two answers exactly the first question the number raises (whose result, from what) and adds the so-what: acquisition cost falling, not applause requested.
- The order of information runs number, then owner, then mechanism. The baseline ran feelings, then promise, then nothing.
- One number, one gap. The open question is "what are they doing to get that", and the body exists to answer it.
- No adjectives touch the number. 669,000 stated dryly is the register; the precision itself does the persuading.

## Calibration: when the claim leads instead

The skill does not mandate numbers. Will's highest-engagement post in the same period opens with no number at all, verbatim:

> Ask a founder about storytelling and they'll point you to their marketing team.
>
> That tells you everything.

There is no metric in the source for that idea, and the voltage is a contradiction in founder behavior, so a claim-led opener through the parent `hooks` skill is correct. Forcing a number into that post would have meant inventing one, which the provenance law refuses, or promoting a trivial one, which the surprise test refuses.

The fork in one line: 669,000 led because it broke the reader's model; the storytelling post led with a claim because its mechanism was the surprise and no honest number existed. Both are data discipline. Only one of them shows a digit.
