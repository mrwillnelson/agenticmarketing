# Rubric anchors

What low, middle, and high look like on each axis. Read this when a sub-score feels arbitrary. All examples are invented.

## Thesis clarity (15%)

- 20: a topic. "Fundraising in 2026."
- 50: a stance without an edge. "Founders should prepare more before fundraising."
- 85: one falsifiable belief. "Your data room should be finished before your first coffee meeting, because the second meeting is where deals die."

## Source truth (20%)

- 20: no quote, or a paraphrase presented as a quote, or a quote that does not support the thesis.
- 50: a verbatim quote that gestures at the thesis but would not convince a skeptic on its own.
- 85: a verbatim line of 40+ characters that carries the thesis by itself. "We had eleven second meetings and zero term sheets, and every single one stalled on the same missing document."

## Reader tension (15%)

- 20: generic encouragement. "Keep going, consistency pays off."
- 50: a real problem stated flatly, no stakes or surprise.
- 85: the reader recognizes a fear or contradiction they already carry. "The thing you are polishing is not the thing killing your deals."

## Hook potential (15%)

- 20: a topic label. "Some thoughts on onboarding."
- 50: an interesting fact that gives away its own ending.
- 85: a concrete moment with one open question. "The candidate we rejected in March runs our best product line now."

## Originality (15%)

- 20: a platitude in the author's niche, or an angle the author published recently. Duplicated angle counts even when the words differ.
- 50: a familiar idea with a genuinely new supporting story.
- 85: a claim the reader has not seen argued this way, grounded in the speaker's specific experience.

## Narrative fit (10%)

- 20: fights the author's pillars or contradicts their stated narrative.
- 50: adjacent to a pillar; publishable but off-arc.
- 85: lands squarely in a pillar and moves the larger narrative forward.
- Empty pack: score on internal coherence only and flag the score as provisional.

## Audience clarity (10%)

- 20: "everyone" or "founders".
- 50: a segment with one qualifier. "Early-stage founders."
- 85: a reader you could picture. "Bootstrapped agency owners deciding whether their first hire is a PM or another builder."

## Weighted total

candidateScore = 0.15(thesis clarity) + 0.20(source truth) + 0.15(reader tension) + 0.15(hook potential) + 0.15(originality) + 0.10(narrative fit) + 0.10(audience clarity), rounded to the nearest integer.

A candidate below 60 total is usually not worth drafting. Report it anyway if extracted; the filter downstream decides.
