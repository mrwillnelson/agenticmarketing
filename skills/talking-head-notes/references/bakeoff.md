# Bake-off: the teleprompter script versus the shot sheet

One idea, two ways to film it. The idea is real: a post by Will Nelson, Founder of 64stories.com, published on LinkedIn on 2026-03-12 (11 reactions, 2 comments, 127 words). Below it: the full-sentence script most founders would write for the video version and then read stiffly off a teleprompter, and the one-card shot sheet this skill builds instead. Both artifacts were written for this comparison. Beats not present in the source post are labeled synthetic.

## The source post (verbatim)

> In the last 90 days, 64stories has grown an established CEO’s LinkedIn account by 500% by doing something really simple.
>
> We share company milestones through the CEO’s voice.
>
> Every two weeks I hop on a call with them to propose post ideas we believe will perform well.
>
> We discuss them together, as if we’re on the same side of the table.
>
> We shape them together – ensuring they’re true, authentic, and aligned to the story we’re telling.
>
> Then we publish and wait.
>
> If something starts to take off, they get boosted as Thought Leader Ads.
>
> All likes and engagements from the posts are scraped and sent to sales to nurture.
>
> Ideate > Capture > Create > Measure > Amplify

## The baseline: a full-sentence script

> In the last ninety days, we grew an established CEO's LinkedIn account by five hundred percent, and today I want to walk you through exactly how we did it. The core of the approach is sharing company milestones through the CEO's own voice. Every two weeks, I get on a call with the CEO to propose post ideas that we believe will perform well. We discuss them together, as if we're on the same side of the table, and we shape them together to make sure they're true, authentic, and aligned to the story we're telling. Then we publish and wait. If a post starts to take off, we boost it as a Thought Leader Ad. And finally, all of the likes and engagement get scraped and sent to sales to nurture. That's the whole system: ideate, capture, create, measure, amplify.

Nothing in this script is wrong. Read from a teleprompter by a founder who reads stiffly, all of it is wrong: "and today I want to walk you through" is written connective tissue no one says out loud, the 40-word third sentence has no breath in it, and the eyes track left to right on every line. The delivery problem is not fixable in the edit, because every second of a wooden read is wooden.

## The shot sheet this skill builds

```
HOOK (memorize, word for word):
"We grew a CEO's LinkedIn 500% in 90 days."

BEATS (glance, then eyes back to the lens):
- the simple part: milestones, in the CEO's voice, not ours
- every two weeks: the call, ideas on the table, same side of it
- shape together, then publish and wait
- takeoff = Thought Leader Ads; likes scraped, sent to sales
- [synthetic] the first post that took off, and what changed after

LAND: end on the loop running by itself now: ideate, capture,
create, measure, amplify.

PICKUPS: re-enter at any beat line. Flub mid-beat, return to that
trigger phrase, not the top. The "publish and wait" boundary is the
cleanest cut point if the take splits in two.
```

Every beat except the labeled one is lifted from the post's own specifics: the 500% and 90 days, the biweekly call, "same side of the table", publish and wait, Thought Leader Ads, the scrape-to-sales handoff. The synthetic beat exists to show the labeling convention: a first-takeoff moment would be a strong fourth-position beat, but the source post does not contain one, so on a real engagement it either comes from the founder on the pre-call or it comes off the card.

## Why the shot sheet wins on camera

- One memorized line instead of 150. The founder drills ten words until they are automatic, and the first 3 seconds, the only part that cannot be improvised, come out clean. The other 40 seconds come out in whatever words the founder's mouth picks, which is what a viewer reads as a person talking.
- The bullets carry the substance so improvisation never has to. "Every two weeks: the call, ideas on the table" cannot drift into vagueness, because the specifics are on the card. The founder is never inventing facts on camera, only phrasing.
- The landing direction survives contact with the take. However beat four came out, "end on the loop running by itself" still works as an instruction. The baseline's scripted closer only works if the preceding 140 words came out as written, and they never do.
- The pickup list makes retakes cheap. On the teleprompter script, a stumble at word 90 restarts a 150-word read, and every restart reads stiffer than the last. On the shot sheet, a stumble inside beat three costs one beat.
- The failure modes are asymmetric. The shot sheet's failure is rambling, which the edit cuts. The script's failure is woodenness, which nothing cuts.

## When the baseline would win anyway

If this video were a product walkthrough with exact click paths, or the 500% claim needed legally careful wording, or this founder happened to be a strong natural reader, the full script is the right tool and `short-form-script` should build it properly. The bake-off is not script versus notes in general. It is script versus notes for a founder whose reads come out embalmed, which is the founder this skill exists for.
