# Bake-off: baseline response vs with-skill response

One press request, two responses, and the reasons a journalist picks one and deletes the other. The request below is synthetic, written for this comparison. The with-skill response is written as Will Nelson, Founder of 64stories.com, would answer it, and every number in it is real and comes from his actual material.

## The request (synthetic)

> **Outlet:** B2B marketing trade publication
> **Deadline:** 24 hours
> **Query:** I'm writing a piece on AI in founder-led content. How are founders using AI in their content without losing authenticity? Looking for specific practices, not "keep the human in the loop" platitudes. Include your name, title, and company for attribution.

## Baseline response (no skill)

> Great question! AI is definitely transforming how founders approach content, and we think the key is balance. Tools can dramatically increase output, but founders need to make sure they stay true to their core values and unique perspective, because at the end of the day authenticity is the currency of modern brand building. In our experience, the founders who win are the ones who use AI to augment rather than replace the human touch. At 64stories we've built a process that helps founders use AI while keeping their voice front and center, and we'd be happy to share more or walk you through a demo. Feel free to reach out anytime, and thanks for covering this important topic!

### Why this gets deleted

- The first sentence is filler. Nothing in "AI is definitely transforming how founders approach content" can be printed under anyone's name, because everyone's name fits under it.
- Every claim is a hedge: "the key is balance," "augment rather than replace," "stay true to your values." The journalist explicitly asked for practices, not platitudes, and got platitudes.
- Zero numbers, zero examples. There is nothing to verify and nothing to cite.
- It pitches. "Happy to walk you through a demo" converts the response from a source offer into a sales email, and journalists delete sales emails.
- No attribution line. The journalist would have to write back to ask who this even is, and they will not.

## With-skill response (as Will Nelson would answer)

> Founders keep authenticity by treating voice as a measurement, not a vibe. Before AI touches a draft we fingerprint the founder's real writing: from one 33-post set we measured a median post of 79 words, sentences averaging 18 words, and 1.8 contractions per 100 words, and every draft gets held to those numbers. The second practice is sourcing: drafts start from stories the founder actually told on a call, never from a blank prompt, so the raw material is theirs even when tooling shapes it. Run both and the output stays indistinguishable from the founder's own hand; one client's founder posts drew 669,000 impressions in 28 days on exactly this system.
>
> Will Nelson, Founder of 64stories, which runs founder-led content programs on LinkedIn; one CEO account grew 500 percent in 90 days under this process.

### Why this gets selected

- Sentence one answers the question and survives alone. "Founders keep authenticity by treating voice as a measurement, not a vibe" prints cleanly with a name under it, which is the entire job of the first sentence.
- It gives the specific practices the journalist asked for, with numbers a fact-checker can query: 79 words, 18-word sentences, 1.8 contractions per 100 words, 669,000 impressions in 28 days. Every figure comes from Will's real material, nothing composited or estimated.
- The sentences are distinctive. "A measurement, not a vibe" and "never from a blank prompt" are quotable phrasings in Will's register; journalists lift distinctive sentences and cut generic ones.
- No pitch anywhere in the body. The company name appears once, in the credential line, attached to a verifiable result instead of an offer.
- It is under 150 words including the credential, scannable in the ten seconds this response will get, and complete: the journalist can quote it without a follow-up email.
- It stays inside genuine standing. Will runs this process; he can defend every sentence on a follow-up call, which is what hard rule 2 protects.

## The pattern to reuse

Answer first, prove second, credential last. The baseline fails on all three: it answers nothing, proves nothing, and spends its credibility on a pitch. The with-skill version wins not because it is longer or cleverer but because every sentence is either printable or verifiable, and the journalist's job is to find exactly those sentences fast.
