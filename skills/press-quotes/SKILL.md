# Press Quotes

Journalists post requests for sources every day, and a founder with real experience can win those quotes. The prize is not the logo; it is placement in outlets your buyers actually read. A quote in a niche trade publication your ICP subscribes to beats a mention in tier-1 tech press that nobody in your pipeline will ever see. This skill covers the whole loop: deciding whether the channel is worth the hours at all, picking platforms, triaging requests daily, writing responses journalists lift verbatim, and logging what converts.

## Hard rules

1. Run the ROI gate in step 1 before any platform setup. Signing up is free; the cadence is not. An unanswered pipeline of requests is worse than no pipeline.
2. Never answer a request outside the user's genuine expertise. A bad quote in print outlives the placement and attaches the user's name to a topic they cannot defend on a follow-up call.
3. Never invent numbers, client stories, or credentials. Every claim in a response must be citable from the user's real material. If no real proof exists for the request, skip the request.
4. The first sentence of every response must be liftable as a standalone quote. If a journalist could not print sentence one with only a name attached, rewrite it.
5. No pitching the product inside a response. The one-line credential at the end carries the company name, and that is all the company gets.

## Procedure

1. Gate the channel honestly before anything else. Ask what the user can actually sustain:
   - Roughly 5 hours a week across platforms: a realistic 3 to 10 placements a month once the rhythm is established.
   - Roughly 1 hour a week: 0 to 2 placements a month, most months landing at 0.
   - Less than that, or a cadence that will collapse in three weeks: recommend skipping this channel entirely and putting the hour into commenting on posts your ICP already reads instead. Say this plainly. Do not soften the math to keep the user excited.
   Only proceed to setup after the user picks a cadence they can hold.
2. Set up platforms against the ICP, not against platform popularity. Read `my-story/icp.md` and ask one question: where do the outlets your buyers read go for quotes? Connectively skews broad consumer and business press, Qwoted skews finance and B2B trades, Featured feeds roundup-style pieces, Help a B2B Writer is B2B marketing and SaaS, SourceBottle skews ANZ and lifestyle. Pick the one or two that overlap the user's buyers. Set alert keywords from the topics in `my-story/pillars.md`, plus the user's industry terms, so requests arrive pre-filtered.
3. Triage daily, 15 minutes, not more. Kill non-fits fast:
   - Wrong beat: the topic is outside the user's genuine standing, per hard rule 2.
   - Content-farm outlets: no real audience, exists to sell links. A placement there is worth nothing.
   - Free-consulting fishing: "please detail your exact process for X" with no outlet named. That is a brief, not a press request.
   Rank what survives on two axes: how much the outlet's audience overlaps the ICP, and how strong the user's genuine standing is to answer. Answer the top one or two. Skipping a mediocre fit to answer nothing is a fine outcome for the day.
4. Write the quoteable response:
   - Answer the actual question in the first sentence. Not context, not throat-clearing, the answer.
   - Include one specific number or one concrete example from the user's real experience. One is enough; proof discipline per hard rule 3.
   - Write it in the user's measured voice from `my-story/voice-model.md` and `my-story/lexicon.md`. Journalists quote distinctive sentences and cut generic ones; a response that sounds like everyone else's gets skimmed and dropped.
   - Shape: 2 to 4 sentences of quotable material, then a one-line credential (name, title, company, one proof point). Total under 150 words. Journalists scan dozens of responses; short and sharp wins.
   - Send inside the window. Hours matter; most requests fill from the first responses in, not the best response overall. A good answer in 2 hours beats a great one in 2 days.
5. Log every response and every placement. Track which platforms and which beats actually convert to print, and which placements got noticed by buyers. Feed a short dated note into `my-story/what-worked.md` so the next triage cycle weights the platforms and beats that earn placements, not the ones that feel prestigious.

For a full worked comparison of a generic response against a with-skill response to the same request, see [references/bakeoff.md](references/bakeoff.md).

## Personalization

Reads from `my-story/`:

- `voice-model.md`: the rhythm the response is written in; distinctive cadence is what gets quoted.
- `lexicon.md`: the user's own vocabulary and the "never says" list; both binding in responses.
- `icp.md`: who the buyers are, which drives platform choice and outlet ranking in steps 2 and 3.
- `narrative.md`: the through-line the credential and examples should reinforce, never contradict.
- `pillars.md`: the topics the user has standing on; the source of alert keywords and the boundary for hard rule 2.

With an empty pack the skill still runs: the ROI gate needs no files, and triage can work from the user's stated expertise. But responses will read generic, platform choice becomes a guess, and the expertise boundary has to be re-asked every request. Recommend running `voice-pack` once; `icp.md` and `pillars.md` are the two files that change results here the most.

## Examples

Bad: "That's a great question, and honestly it depends on the founder's goals, but in general we've found that authenticity comes down to staying true to your values while embracing new tools."
Better: "Founders keep authenticity by treating voice as a measurement, not a vibe. We fingerprint a founder's real writing, sentence length and contraction rate and the words they never use, and hold every draft to those numbers."
Why: the bad one hedges for a full sentence before saying nothing a journalist can print; the better one's first sentence stands alone as a quote with a name under it.

Bad: "At 64stories, our AI content platform helps founders scale authentic content. Readers can book a demo at 64stories.com to see how it works for their brand."
Better: "Will Nelson is the founder of 64stories, which runs founder-led content programs; one client's founder posts drew 669,000 impressions in 28 days."
Why: the bad one pitches, which journalists delete along with the whole response; the better one is a credential line that earns the company name by attaching it to a verifiable result.

Bad: "Our clients typically see around 3x engagement within the first month of using this approach."
Better: "One CEO account we run grew 500 percent in 90 days, and the whole system is the founder telling company stories in his own voice."
Why: "typically around 3x" is an invented composite no one can stand behind in print; the better one is a single real number from a single real engagement.

Bad triage: answering a request from a domain-rated-nothing "business tips" blog because it is the easiest win of the day.
Better triage: skipping it and spending the same 20 minutes on a trade-publication request where the user's buyers actually read the outlet, or answering nothing today.
Why: placements are only worth the audience attached to them; an easy placement nobody in the pipeline sees costs time and returns a screenshot.

## Self-check

- Did the ROI gate run first, and did the user commit to a cadence they can hold? If they cannot, did I recommend skipping the channel instead of setting it up anyway?
- Is this request inside the user's genuine expertise, and could they defend every claim on a follow-up call?
- Is every number and example real and citable from the user's material, with nothing invented or averaged into existence?
- Does the first sentence answer the question and survive alone as a printed quote?
- Is the response 2 to 4 quotable sentences plus a one-line credential, under 150 words, in the user's measured voice?
- Is the company name confined to the credential line?
- Is the response going out inside the request window?
- After a placement lands or a beat goes cold, did the note reach `my-story/what-worked.md`?

## Related skills

- `founder-pitch` is the outbound sibling: pitching your own story to a journalist who asked for nothing. This skill is inbound: answering a request a journalist already posted. Different motion, different patience.
- `voice-match` shapes the response itself; run it on any response that reads clean but generic, because generic is exactly what gets cut.
- `what-worked` owns the learning loop; the placement notes from step 5 feed the same file it maintains.
- If the ROI gate fails, the recommended alternative is a commenting habit on posts the ICP reads, which costs the same hour and compounds in public.
