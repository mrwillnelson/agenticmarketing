# Bake-off: stuffed baseline versus with-skill placement

Same seed, same target keyword, two treatments. Read this when you need to see the difference between placement that ranks and placement that repels.

Seed: the thesis of a real Will Nelson LinkedIn post (141 reactions, 35 comments): your story is your strategy, made legible. A founder who cannot clearly explain what the company does usually does not have a clear strategy; the two problems are the same problem.

Primary keyword: "founder storytelling"
Secondaries: "founder-led content", "company narrative", "explain what your company does"
Primary question for answer engines: "why does founder storytelling matter?"

## (a) The stuffed baseline

> ## Founder Storytelling: Why Founder Storytelling Matters for Startups
>
> Founder storytelling is one of the most underrated growth levers for startups. Many founders think founder storytelling is a marketing task, but founder storytelling is actually a strategy task. If you want to improve your founder storytelling, you need to understand that the story you tell reveals whether you have made the hard strategic decisions about what your company is and what it is not.

What is wrong, placement by placement:

- The subhead is a query in a costume. "Founder Storytelling: Why Founder Storytelling Matters for Startups" makes no claim, repeats the phrase twice, and tells every human reader this page was built for a crawler. Modern retrieval does not reward the repetition, and the reader it repels never shares, links, or dwells, which are the signals that do rank.
- Four exact matches in 76 words. That is one placement per 19 words against a ceiling of one per 150. Density this high reads as spam to readers and increasingly to ranking systems trained on human preference.
- Every sentence exists to host the phrase. Read any of them aloud: "Many founders think founder storytelling is a marketing task" is a sentence nobody says. The argument (story reveals strategy) is present but buried under its own packaging.
- Zero quotable units. An answer engine looking for a passage that answers "why does founder storytelling matter" finds only sentences that restate the question's noun. There is nothing self-contained to cite.

## (b) The with-skill version

> ## Storytelling forces clarity
>
> Ask a founder about storytelling and they'll point you to their marketing team. That tells you everything. If you can't clearly explain what your company does, you probably don't have a clear strategy. The two problems are the same problem.
>
> This is why founder storytelling matters more than founders expect: the story is a diagnostic. When you try to explain your strategy to someone who knows nothing about your space, you discover what you don't understand. The gaps in your narrative reveal the gaps in your strategy.
>
> Your story is your strategy, made legible.

Why this ranks AND reads, placement by placement:

- The subhead "Storytelling forces clarity" makes a claim and carries the argument's spine on its own. It contains the topic term without the full phrase; retrieval matches it against variant queries, and a skimmer reading only subheads still gets the argument. One subhead slot used, voice intact.
- The first 100 words open with the source's own hook and land "founder", "storytelling", and "explain what your company does" (a secondary, verbatim as a natural clause) inside the fold without any sentence reading as placed. The load-bearing early slot is fully covered at a density of roughly one intentional placement per 100 words across the excerpt, under the ceiling once the full article's length is counted.
- One exact match of the primary phrase in the whole excerpt, inside the answer passage where it earns its seat. Everything else is semantic variation: "story", "narrative", "strategy". Variants outnumber the exact match, so the piece covers the query space without repeating itself.
- The middle paragraph is the quotable answer passage. Three sentences, self-contained, primary phrase in its first sentence, and it directly answers "why does founder storytelling matter": because the story is a diagnostic and narrative gaps reveal strategy gaps. An AI answer engine can lift that paragraph standalone and attribute it. No FAQ block was added; the passage sits where the essay naturally pauses to say what it means.
- The closer "Your story is your strategy, made legible" is the strongest plain sentence in the piece and it fills the closing-paragraph slot with the semantic core ("story", "strategy") rather than a stuffed CTA. It is also the line a human quotes when sharing, which is the distribution the stuffed version can never buy.
- Integrity check: delete the keyword map and reread. Every sentence in (b) survives because every sentence argues. In (a), removing the map leaves almost nothing standing.

The lesson: (a) optimized the page and lost the reader; (b) optimized the argument's natural surfaces and kept both. Discoverability came from covering the load-bearing slots with language the author would have used anyway, plus one citable passage, not from repetition.
