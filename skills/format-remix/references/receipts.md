## The extracted skeleton: the teardown format

- Hook pattern: subject hook. A named outsider achieved an enviable, numerically stated result, and the reader is promised the copyable method before the subject is introduced.
- Beats in order: subject and result with exact numbers, the mechanism (what they do instead of the obvious default), why it works (the second-order effects the surface tactic feeds), invitation (the reader nominates the next subject).
- Rhythm profile: one-line hook, then paragraphs of one to two long sentences each. The shortest lines sit at the open and the close.
- Engine: receipts plus vicarious ambition. The reader wants the outcome, the exact numbers make it credible, and the invitation converts wanting it into participating.

## Why this skeleton passes rule 2

Nothing in it names Jacob, followers, live sessions, or AI agents. The same skeleton could shape a teardown of a chef's booking system or a newsletter operator's referral loop. If your extraction still contains "5,000 to 52,000", that is content, not structure. Keep abstracting until it could describe a hundred posts.

## Before reusing it

Rule 3 still applies. The teardown format needs three things the user genuinely has:

- A subject whose result the user's audience actually envies, with numbers the user can state exactly and source publicly.
- A mechanism the user understands well enough to explain the second-order effects, not just describe the surface tactic.
- Willingness to honor the invitation beat. Will's close only works because he answers the comments. An invitation nobody follows up on reads as bait.

Missing any of the three, refuse the remix and say which one is missing.
