# Engagement to Pipeline

Every post leaves a trail: the people who reacted, commented, reshared, or quietly checked the profile afterward. That trail is the warmest list the user will ever have, and the easiest one to burn. This skill scores who engaged, reads how deeply they engaged, and routes each person into a next step that continues a conversation they started. Nobody gets pitched, and the weekly result feeds back into `my-story/what-worked.md` so content and pipeline learn from each other.

## Hard rules

1. Reference the specific content they engaged with, always. A message that could follow any post follows no post. Name the post, and when they commented, name their comment.
2. The DM continues a conversation they started. It does not start yours. If they commented, reply in their thread before anything moves to their inbox.
3. Never a pitch as a first touch, for any tier, ever. No offer, no "quick call", no "what we do", no link to a deck. The first touch asks nothing for the sender.
4. One follow-up maximum. Silence is an answer. Mark the person as quiet in the notes and move on; a second nudge converts a warm engager into someone who avoids the user's posts.
5. Comments from peers and vendors are community, not pipeline. A fellow ghostwriter, an agency owner, or someone selling into the user's audience gets a warm reply and an honest label. Never count them in pipeline numbers.
6. Never automate the DMs themselves. Collecting and scoring the list can be assisted; the messages get written for one named person and sent by a human. A sequence tool wearing a first name is still a sequence tool.
7. Tier against `my-story/icp.md` using the same A/B/C/D tiers as `connections-icp-match`, with the same discipline: disqualifiers first, tier down when unsure, no numeric scores. If `icp.md` is empty, draft it with the user before scoring (see Personalization).
8. Engagement data stays local. Names, titles, and comment text never leave the session for enrichment, search, or any external tool.

## Procedure

1. Collect the engagers. After each post, or weekly in one batch, the user pastes or exports who engaged: reactions, comments with their text, reshares, and profile views if their plan shows them. Record which post each signal came from; the post is what makes the follow-up specific. If comment text is missing, ask for it before routing commenters.
2. Score each person against `my-story/icp.md` into the tiers `connections-icp-match` defines:
   - A: title matches the ICP role and the company signal matches the ICP company type.
   - B: one strong signal, not both.
   - C: adjacent. Influences the buyer, or the row is too thin to call.
   - D: not ICP, or hits a disqualifier. Also flag peers and vendors here for the community column, whatever their title says.
3. Classify engagement depth, separate from tier:
   - Substantive comment: adds a story, a disagreement, or a specific detail. The strongest single-post signal.
   - Reshare with their own framing: they staked their name on the idea. Treat like a substantive comment.
   - Short comment ("Great post", an emoji, a tag): a reaction with punctuation.
   - Reaction: real but shallow.
   - Profile view after a post: a lurk. They came looking; they did not knock.
   - Repeat engagement across multiple posts is the highest-intent signal on LinkedIn and outranks any single touch. Check every engager against previous weeks' lists before routing.
4. Route each person by tier plus depth:
   - A-tier, substantive comment or framed reshare: reply in their thread first, with something that extends their point. Then, and only then, send a connection request or DM that references their specific comment and continues that exact conversation. Ask about them; offer nothing.
   - A-tier, reaction or lurk only: add to the warm list. Engage on their content first, two or three real comments over a week or two, before any outreach. The DM, when it comes, references that thread.
   - B-tier, repeat engager: they just gave the second signal. Move them onto the warm list and start engaging on their content.
   - B-tier, single signal: watchlist. Reply in-thread if they commented, then wait for the second signal.
   - C-tier: reply warmly in-thread if they commented. No outreach.
   - Peers and vendors: community column. Reply generously; they carry reach. Never route them to pipeline.
   - D-tier: nothing.
5. Write the weekly pipeline note and append it as a dated block to `my-story/what-worked.md`, newest at the top: new A-tier engagers and which post pulled them, conversations opened this week, conversations that earned a call, and the community count labeled as community. Keep it under ten lines. This is how `what-worked` learns which content attracts buyers, not just reach.

To see the whole motion run against a real post, read [references/bakeoff.md](references/bakeoff.md): a 12-person engager list on a real 141-reaction post, the spray-and-pray baseline, and the with-skill routing table with two example DMs.

## Personalization

Reads from `my-story/`:

- `icp.md`: the tiering rubric. Titles, company type and size, disqualifiers. If empty or missing, do not score against an invented ICP; run the 5-question ICP draft from `connections-icp-match` with the user first, write the answers to `icp.md`, then score.
- `narrative.md`: what the content is arguing overall. Use it to judge whether a comment engages the thesis or just the surface, and to keep DMs continuing the right conversation.
- `what-worked.md`: read the latest block to see which posts have been pulling A-tier engagers; write the weekly pipeline note here.

With an empty pack the skill still classifies depth and applies every hard rule, but tiering blocks on the ICP draft above, and routing suggestions come back marked "confirm tier with user" until `icp.md` exists.

## Examples

Bad DM: "Hi Priya, thanks for commenting on my post! I help B2B founders turn their story into pipeline. Do you have 15 minutes this week to see if we could help Loftwing?"
Better: "Priya, your comment on the storytelling post stayed with me. The one-liner fight being a roadmap fight in disguise is the whole argument in one sentence. Did you two ever land the one-liner, or is it still open?"
Why: the bad one converts her comment into his meeting and would read the same to all 35 commenters; the better one continues the conversation she started, quotes her actual point, and asks about her company, not his calendar.

Bad read: "The post got 141 reactions and 35 comments, so it worked. Message everyone who liked it."
Better: "141 reactions, and inside them: two A-tier founders left substantive comments, one A-tier CEO reshared with her own framing, and one B-tier VP reacted for the second week running. Four people to route; the rest is reach."
Why: the bad read treats volume as intent and burns the list; the better read separates the four buyer signals from the applause and routes only those.

Bad label: "18 comments this week, strong pipeline signal."
Better: "18 comments: 3 from ICP-fit founders, 9 from peers and content folks (community, good for reach), 2 from vendors pitching in the replies, 4 unclear. Pipeline signal is the 3."
Why: peers and vendors talking to each other is a healthy comment section and zero pipeline; counting them as buyers makes the content strategy learn the wrong lesson.

Bad follow-up: "Just bumping this in case it got buried! Would still love to connect."
Better: nothing. She saw it. Log her as quiet in the weekly note and keep showing up in the feed; the next post is the follow-up.
Why: one follow-up was already the maximum; past that, every message spends trust the content earned.

## Self-check

- Does every suggested message name the specific post, and the specific comment when there is one?
- Does every DM continue something they said, and ask nothing for the sender?
- Did anyone, any tier, get a pitch as a first touch? The answer must be no.
- Were repeat engagers checked against previous weeks before routing?
- Are peers and vendors labeled community and excluded from pipeline counts?
- Is any step automating the sending of messages? The answer must be no.
- Did unclear tiers get rounded down, per the `connections-icp-match` discipline?
- Did the weekly note land as a dated block at the top of `my-story/what-worked.md`, with calls and conversations separated from community?

## Related skills

- `connections-icp-match` scores the whole connections graph against the same `icp.md` rubric; this skill scores the people a single post surfaced. Run it first to build the tiers this skill reuses.
- `what-worked` closes the loop: the weekly pipeline note lands in its file, so the next content cycle weights the posts that pulled buyers, not just impressions.
- `linkedin-post` earns the engagement in the first place. No post, no list.
