# Posting Cadence

A cadence is a promise the user makes to their audience with their worst week, not their best one. This skill derives the largest schedule the user can keep on a bad week, ties every slot to a named material source, and defines in advance what happens when a slot is missed. Consistency at 2 posts per week beats bursts of 5 followed by silence, because the audience and the algorithm both price in the silence.

## Hard rules

1. Capacity decides, aspiration does not. The cadence is derived from the audit numbers in step 1, never from what the user wishes they could do or what a guru prescribed.
2. Never prescribe daily posting to someone with no daily material source. Daily output requires daily input: calls being recorded, a stocked idea bank, or a listening routine that surfaces material every day. No daily input, no daily slot.
3. Quality gates are never relaxed to hit cadence. A slot with nothing real produces nothing. Per `meeting-to-post`, "no post today" is a success. The `anti-ai` scan and the user's voice rules apply at full strength on deadline day.
4. Every slot names its source. "Monday post from last week's best call" is a slot. "Write something Tuesday" is not a slot, it is a future guilt object. A slot without a source does not go on the calendar.
5. A missed slot is skipped, never backfilled. Publishing Thursday's post plus a filler makeup post on Friday teaches the audience that half the feed is filler.
6. Two consecutive missed weeks triggers a cadence cut, not a guilt spiral. Drop one slot, keep the schedule, say so plainly. The floor was set too high; that is a measurement error, not a character flaw.
7. Volume goes up only after four consecutive weeks at the current floor, and only one slot at a time, informed by the latest `my-story/what-worked.md` block.
8. Platform norms inform, capacity decides. If a platform "rewards" 5 posts a week and the user can sustain 2, the cadence is 2. An unsustainable 5 becomes 0 by week six.

## Procedure

1. Capacity audit. Get real numbers, not vibes:
   - Calls per week that get recorded or noted. Each one is potential material via `meeting-to-post`. A founder with 6 recorded calls a week has 6 material shots, of which 2 or 3 will typically clear the bar.
   - Minutes per day the user will actually spend on content, on a busy week. 30 minutes a day is one `meeting-to-post` rep, not three drafts.
   - Whether a human reviews before publish. A review step adds a day of latency per post and caps weekly throughput at whatever the reviewer clears.
   - Existing reserves: idea bank depth, evergreen drafts, a listening routine from `my-story/listening-sources.md`.
2. Derive the floor cadence. Take the WORST realistic week from the audit (travel week, launch week, sick kid week) and ask what volume survives it. That number is the cadence. Typical mappings: 1 to 2 recorded calls a week and 15 minutes a day supports 1 post a week; 4 to 6 calls and 30 minutes a day supports 2 to 3; daily slots require the daily inputs in hard rule 2. When in doubt, round down; a floor that is too low gets raised in a month, a floor that is too high collapses.
3. Map every slot to a source. For each slot, write one line: day, post, source. The source is where the material comes from, not what the topic is. Good sources: last week's best call (via `meeting-to-post`), the top item in the idea bank, this month's `what-worked` test, a listening source from `my-story/listening-sources.md`. Balance slots across `my-story/pillars.md` so one pillar does not eat the calendar. If a slot cannot name a source, the cadence is too high; go back to step 2.
4. Write down the misses rule with the cadence, so deadline-day judgment is already made:
   - A slot whose source produced nothing real is skipped. No filler, no recycled platitude, no "just ship something".
   - A skipped slot is never backfilled later in the week.
   - Two consecutive weeks below floor: cut one slot immediately and note the date. Resume the old floor only via step 5.
5. Review monthly against `my-story/what-worked.md`. Check three things: weeks at floor (four consecutive weeks at floor earns the right to add ONE slot, with its own named source), which slots keep getting skipped (a slot skipped 3 of 4 weeks has a broken source; fix the source or delete the slot), and whether the weights suggest reallocating a slot to a stronger pillar or format before adding any volume.

Read [references/bakeoff.md](references/bakeoff.md) to see a "post daily at 9am" plan fail against a floor cadence on the same founder profile.

## Personalization

Reads from `my-story/`:

- `pillars.md`: the pillars to balance slots across in step 3. Empty pack: map slots to sources only and note that pillar balance is unchecked.
- `what-worked.md`: the latest weights block drives the monthly review in step 5 and biases which slot gets added when volume earns an increase. Empty pack: review on completion rate alone and suggest running `what-worked` after the first month of data.
- `listening-sources.md`: candidate material sources for slots beyond calls. Empty pack: slots draw only from calls and the idea bank, which usually caps the floor lower.

The skill works with an empty pack; the audit questions in step 1 supply everything essential. Filling the pack turns "2 posts a week" into "2 posts a week, balanced across your pillars, weighted toward what worked".

## Examples

Bad: "Post every weekday at 9am. Consistency is everything, so show up daily no matter what."
Better: "You have 6 recorded calls and about 30 focused minutes a day. Floor cadence: Monday post from last week's best call, Thursday post from the top idea-bank item. Hold that for four weeks before we discuss a third slot."
Why: the bad one prescribes daily output with no daily material source and no worst-week test; the better one derives volume from the audit and names a source for each slot.

Bad: "You missed Thursday, so publish two posts on Friday to stay on track for the week."
Better: "Thursday's call had no real moment, so Thursday is skipped. Monday's slot is unchanged. One skipped slot is the misses rule working, not a problem to repair."
Why: backfilling converts a quality gate into a quota, and the makeup post is filler by construction.

Bad: "Week two below floor. You need more discipline; block 6am tomorrow and catch up on the backlog."
Better: "That is two consecutive weeks below floor, so the cadence cuts from three slots to two, effective now. Which slot has the weakest source? That one goes."
Why: two missed weeks is evidence the floor was measured wrong; the fix is a smaller promise, not a bigger guilt debt.

Bad: "Engagement is up this month, so let's jump from 2 posts a week to 5 while the momentum is hot."
Better: "Four consecutive weeks at floor, and what-worked shows founder-journey running above median. Add ONE slot: a second founder-journey post, sourced from the idea bank. Re-review in a month."
Why: volume increases are earned one slot at a time after four clean weeks, and the new slot arrives with a source and a pillar, not a hope.

## Self-check

- Is the cadence derived from the worst realistic week in the audit, not the best?
- Does every slot name a material source, with zero "write something" slots?
- Would this cadence survive the user's busiest week on the calendar this quarter?
- If a slot has no daily source, did I make sure it is not a daily slot?
- Is the misses rule written down with the cadence: skip, never backfill, cut after two missed weeks?
- Is the only path to more volume four consecutive weeks at floor plus a what-worked review?
- Did I keep platform norms advisory and capacity decisive?

## Related skills

- `meeting-to-post`: the daily rep that feeds call-sourced slots; its "no post today" rule is this skill's quality gate on deadline day.
- The idea bank: the buffer source for non-call slots and the parking lot for every skipped slot's half-formed material.
- `what-worked`: produces the weights block the monthly review reads; run it before any volume increase.
- `content-audit`: when the user wants to judge past output quality, not schedule future output.
