# Bake-off: "post daily at 9am" vs a floor cadence mapped to sources

Read this when you want to see what the skill changes on a real-shaped input. Same founder profile, two plans: the baseline is the standard advice to post every day, the with-skill version runs the capacity audit and derives a floor cadence with named sources.

## The founder profile

This profile is constructed for demonstration. Will Nelson, Founder of 64stories.com, is real and his posts quoted below are his, verbatim, with their real engagement numbers. The founder in the profile is invented to match the situations Will has published about under his own name.

- B2B founder, sells to ops leaders, does the selling herself.
- 6 recorded calls per week: 4 sales calls, 1 customer check-in, 1 podcast or partner call.
- 30 minutes a day for content, honestly measured. On launch weeks and travel weeks it drops to near zero on 2 or 3 days.
- A human reviews before publish: her cofounder reads every post, usually within a day.
- Idea bank: empty at the start. No listening routine yet. `my-story/` pack is filled.
- History: she has twice started "posting consistently", once at daily and once at 4 per week. Both runs died inside six weeks.

## Baseline: without the skill

The plan she was given, which reads like most cadence advice:

> Post every weekday at 9am. Consistency compounds. Batch your content on Sunday so you never miss a day. Monday: motivation. Tuesday: tips. Wednesday: story. Thursday: hot take. Friday: recap. Show up daily and the algorithm will reward you. If you miss a day, post twice the next day to stay on schedule.

What actually happens, week by week:

1. Weeks 1 and 2 hold, powered by Sunday batching and enthusiasm. Sunday costs three hours she did not budget.
2. Week 3 has a launch. Two slots miss. Per the plan she posts twice on Thursday; one of the two is a recycled tip she does not believe.
3. Week 4, the topic-of-the-day grid is the problem: "Tuesday: tips" is a slot with no source, so Tuesday becomes a staring contest with a blank page inside her 30 minutes. The cofounder review adds a day, so a 9am daily deadline actually means a draft every single evening.
4. Weeks 5 and 6: two consecutive misses become silence, because the plan has no rule for missing except guilt. Run number three ends like the first two.

The baseline plan failed on every audit fact: it ignored the daily-source requirement (she has 6 weekly material shots, not 5 daily ones), ignored the reviewer latency, ignored the worst week, and its misses rule (backfill with doubles) manufactured filler.

## With the skill

Step trace.

1. Capacity audit: 6 recorded calls a week, of which 2 or 3 will clear the `meeting-to-post` bar in a typical week. 30 minutes a day, which is one rep, not three. Human review adds a day per post. No idea bank yet, no listening sources.
2. Floor cadence, derived from the worst week (launch week, 2 or 3 dead days, calls still happen because calls are revenue): 2 posts per week. Not 5, not 3. The calls survive bad weeks; the free evenings do not.
3. Slots mapped to sources:
   - Monday post from last week's best call, drafted via `meeting-to-post` on Thursday or Friday while the call is fresh, through cofounder review over the weekend.
   - Thursday post from the top item in the idea bank; until the bank has items, from the week's second-best call. Every `meeting-to-post` runner-up gets parked in the bank, so the bank fills as a byproduct of the Monday rep.
   - Pillar balance checked against `my-story/pillars.md` monthly, not per week.
4. Misses rule, written into the plan: a slot with no real material is skipped, never backfilled; two consecutive weeks below floor cuts the cadence to 1 post per week, no self-debate.
5. Monthly review against `my-story/what-worked.md`: four consecutive weeks at 2 per week earns consideration of a third slot, which must arrive with its own named source (the likeliest candidate is a listening-sources slot once `my-story/listening-sources.md` has entries).

## Why the floor cadence beats the daily plan

1. Volume matches input. Six recorded calls yield 2 or 3 shippable moments a week. The daily plan demanded 5 outputs from 2 or 3 inputs, and the gap was always going to be filled with filler. Will Nelson's own numbers make the point that volume is not the lever: his 37-word post ("The fastest way to tell a company is drifting.") pulled 51 reactions while a longer essay teaser in the same set pulled 3. One real moment beats five manufactured ones.
2. The worst week sets the promise. The floor survives the launch week untouched, so week 3 produces 2 posts instead of a double-post Thursday and a credibility dent. The daily plan was calibrated to her best week and died on her median one.
3. Every slot has a source, so deadline day is retrieval, not invention. "Monday from last week's best call" starts from a transcript. "Tuesday: tips" starts from a blank page. Her 30 minutes covers the first and not the second.
4. Misses are governed in advance. Skip, never backfill; two bad weeks cut the cadence. The daily plan's only miss-handling was guilt plus makeup doubles, which is how runs one and two ended.
5. The cadence is a system that feeds itself. The Monday rep parks runner-ups in the idea bank, which stocks the Thursday slot, which is exactly the flywheel Will describes running for clients: "Ideate > Capture > Create > Measure > Amplify", from the post where 64stories grew an established CEO's LinkedIn account by 500% in 90 days on a review-gated, capacity-shaped schedule ("Every two weeks I hop on a call with them"), not on daily output.
6. Growth is earned, not hoped. After four clean weeks the review can add one sourced slot. The daily plan had no way to grow because it started at maximum and only had room to fail.
